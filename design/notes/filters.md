# Filter System

Detailed notes moved from CLAUDE.md (2026-10-10); CLAUDE.md keeps a summary and points here.  Update both when behavior changes.


`lib/mb/sound/filter/` contains 16+ filter types (Biquad, FIR, Butterworth, Hilbert, Delay, etc.). Filters implement `#process` / `#reset`. `Filter::Cookbook` provides standard designs (lowpass, highpass, bandpass, etc.).

Graph cookbook filters are state-variable filters (user decision, 2026-10-08): `node.filter(:lowpass, cutoff:, quality:, gain:)`, `filter(150.hz.highpass(quality: 4))`, `filter(800)`, `peq`, `peq_series`, and `bandpass_series` build `Filter::SVF` (`lib/mb/sound/filter/svf.rb`; Simper's linear trapezoidal SVF, kernel `FastFilter.svf` in `fast_filter` sharing `mb_tan_pade` with the four-pole, exact Ruby mirror `SVF.process_ruby`) with the cookbook's types and parameter meanings (plus `:bandpass_skirt`, peak gain Q, in both structures), so static responses match the biquad (analytic within ~1e-8, float32 renders usually bit-identical).  Its states are integrator states, so cutoff/Q/gain may move on any sample: a cutoff diving toward 0 Hz gives no DC bump (the biquad's 10 ms DC reached 4.8 at Q 3 in the old linear elastic tween), and audio-rate cutoff FM stays bounded (the biquad's output went to infinity at a 2 kHz wobble, Q 4).  `gain:` may be a node with the SVF.  `structure: :biquad` (the only switch; the user declined a global one) builds the old `Filter::Cookbook` through the same path as before (kept for its thumps, e.g. drums); raw `Filter::Biquad`/`Cookbook` objects used directly are unchanged.  ns/sample (kernels, 512-sample buffers): constant params SVF 14 vs `dynamic_biquad` 32 (it recomputes cos/sin every sample), modulated 25 vs 28, scalar static 14 vs `Biquad#process` 12.  Measurements and renders: `/app/tmp/listening/svf_default/`.

(The frequencies in this paragraph and the next are the hardware tuning's; the default since 2026-10-17 is the musical tuning, see Cutoff tuning below.)  `sig.lp4(cutoff, resonance: 0..1)` (aliases `four_pole`, `lowpass4`; also `filter(:lp4, cutoff:, resonance:)`) is a CEM3379-style 4-pole lowpass (`GraphNode::FourPole` over `Filter::FourPole`; zero-delay-feedback OTA cascade in the `fast_filter` extension, exact Ruby mirror `FourPole.process_ruby`, own tan/tanh/2^x approximations): cutoff (Hz, a Pitch, or a node such as `v.cutoff(...)`) and resonance read per sample; -12 dB at the cutoff, 24 dB/octave; like the SQ-80 it never self-oscillates by default (resonance 1 = k 3.9, +37 dB peak) and keeps its bass (-6 dB at full resonance, `compensation: 0.375`; 0 gives -14 dB); `self_oscillate: true` (its own curve, `FourPole.self_oscillate_gain`, kernel curves 2/3: the chosen curve compressed into 0..0.9 reaching k 4 at 0.9, then k = 4 + 1 × x² up to `SELF_OSCILLATE_K` 5, so oscillation starts at 0.9 and its level grows about linearly to ~0.22 peak; starts from silence; was k 4.3 with onset 0.74 until 2026-10-07), `mode:` :lp2/:bp2/:bp4/:hp2/:hp4 taps.  `resonance_curve: :db` (default since 2026-10-07; `:linear` is the round-1 k = r × k_max) makes the gain at the cutoff linear in dB (-12 to +33.8 dB, closed form `FourPole.resonance_curve`; peak +14.5 dB at 0.5 vs +7.5 linear; user-approved 2026-10-07).  Retune values written for the linear curve with `FourPole.db_resonance(linear)` (same loop gain; 0.3 -> 0.21, 0.5 -> 0.33, 0.7 -> 0.47, 0.75 -> 0.51, 0.9 -> 0.68, 0.95 -> 0.79; inverse `linear_resonance`).  Resonant sweeps of a low saw step through single harmonics while the cutoff is low (the narrow peak, not a glitch; detuned pairs make it irregular).  `drive:` (level; saturates above ~1/drive) with `drive_mode:` `:input` (default, tanh on the cascade input), `:stages` (every OTA stage's current plus the input, Zavalishin's one-step secant method), or `:feedback` (`clip: :soft`/`:hard`; only the resonance feedback y4 - c x clips, the MS-20's diode-clipper idea on our 4-pole, passband clean); the last two default to drive 1.  `quality:` instead of `resonance:` (a number or node, e.g. `v.quality(4)`) maps a 2-pole Q to the same gain at the cutoff; in voices `v.reso(0.6)` (alias `resonance_amount`) follows CC 71 (base at 64, 0 at 0, 1 at 127; `v.resonance` is the raw CC 71 Q multiplier).  ns/sample: linear 20, :input 28, :feedback 34-37, :stages 61 (+22 with a per-sample resonance node on the dB curve); ~0.16% of realtime per mono filter at 128-sample buffers.  Renders and measurements: `/app/tmp/listening/four_pole/` (README rounds 1 and 2).

`sig.diode(cutoff, resonance:)` (alias `diode_ladder`; `lp4(mode: :diode)`, `filter(:diode, ...)`) is a TB-303-style diode ladder (`FastFilter.diode_ladder`, exact mirror `FourPole.diode_process_ruby`; from the `research-classic-synths` prototype): four coupled one-pole stages (tridiagonal, open loop 1/D(s), D = s⁴ + 7s³ + 15s² + 10s + 1), TPT solved per sample (Thomas algorithm, linear in the loop input, so cutoff and resonance move every sample), oscillating at k = 901/49 = 18.39 at the cutoff.  Same knobs as lp4 (user: consistency): lp4's curves scaled by K/4 (same distance from oscillation per knob position, same `self_oscillate` onset 0.9), its own `:db` curve (gain at the peak frequency -25.3 to +32.3 dB, linear in dB), compensation, drive `:input`/`:feedback` (`:stages` raises).  `normalize: true` (default; user decision 2026-10-09: easy switching between lp4 and diode, not hardware fidelity; kernel argument 12) matches it to lp4 at the same settings: the stages' frequency × `FourPole.diode_cutoff_scale(k)` (m = 1 + 1.8585 (1 - x)/(1 + 2.1x + 10.6x²), x = k/K: 2.86 at r 0, 2.58 at 0.1, 2.14 at 0.2, 1.65 at 0.3, 1.31 at 0.4, 1.06 at 0.6, 1 at the oscillation edge) keeps the -12 dB-below-DC point at lp4's at r 0 (-12 dB at the cutoff, -3 dB at 0.28 × it vs lp4's 0.43; slopes ~8/11/16/21 dB/oct vs 16/21/24, brighter above) and puts the resonant peak at lp4's frequency from r 0.3 up (within 30 cents; round 3, 2026-10-09: the user heard lp4 a bit brighter at r 0.1-0.4, where round 2 matched the -12 dB point (b 14.4, c 4.85) and the diode's broader peak sat up to 450 cents low; now its -12 dB point is 0.3-0.45 octaves above lp4's at r 0.1-0.3, and a drive-1.5 saw sweep's 0.5-2 kHz bands are 1-5.5 dB under lp4's instead of 3-8.5 at r 0.1-0.4); `diode_compensation(k, k4, c)` replaces the compensation c by c' = ((1 + k)(1 + c k4)/(1 + k4) - 1)/k (k4 = `lp4_loop_gain` at the same knob), so the DC gain equals lp4's at every resonance, compensation, and curve (round 3: the plain c left the diode 1-2 dB quieter from r 0.1 up, -7.8 dB of bass at full resonance vs lp4's -6; now fundamentals within 0.3 dB, RMS within 0.5, and the resonant harmonic under drive 1.5 is still 14 dB over lp4's at r 0.9: acid stays acidic); and `diode_headroom(k)` (1 + (K/4 - 1) x²) lifts the input saturator so self-oscillation matches lp4's level within 0.02 dB (it was 13.25 dB under: the ladder passes 1/K at the resonant frequency, lp4 1/4; `:feedback` drive matched already); `quality:` then gives lp4's knob.  DC through self-oscillation (round 3 check of a swell 0.8→1 that looked offset): running means over whole input periods stay under 5e-4 for both filters (sub-5 Hz power -63 dB re total vs lp4's -50); the waveform's peaks are asymmetric (max/min midpoint up to -0.1, lp4 alike), not offset.  All three are computed only on resonance changes (a per-sample resonance node: ~98 ns/sample vs 91 in round 2, 113 vs 93 with the cutoff moving too).  `normalize: false` is the round-1 ladder (peak at the cutoff at every resonance: -25.3 dB there at r 0, slopes ~14/18/22, self-oscillation ~0.048 peak, plain compensation: -7.8 dB of bass at full resonance).  ns/sample (lp4 vs diode): :input constant 29 vs 34, cutoff per sample 33 vs 48.  Renders and measurements: `/app/tmp/listening/acid/` (round 2 in `round2/`, round 3 in `round3/` with its measurement scripts).

`sig.formant(*vowels, voice: :tenor, morph: 0, shift: 0, width: 1, normalize: true)` (alias `vowel`; `GraphNode::Formant`, 2026-10-11, sound-design-1) is a formant filter: five parallel SVF bandpasses (the `:bandpass` design of `Filter::SVF`, 0 dB peak times the formant's level) at a sung vowel's formants, from the Csound manual's formant table (https://csound.com/docs/manual/MiscFormants.html; voices `:soprano`, `:alto`, `:countertenor`, `:tenor`, `:bass`, vowels `:a :e :i :o :u`; `Formant::VOICES`), or custom vowels as five `[Hz, dB, bandwidth Hz]` triples.  With several vowels `morph:` (0..1 across the list, clamped; a number or node) interpolates each section's log frequency, log Q, and log gain between the two neighboring vowels; `shift:` (semitones, an Interval, or a node: key tracking with `v.number - 60`) multiplies every frequency keeping Q; `width:` (number or node, clamped at 0.01) scales bandwidths.  `normalize: true` scales each vowel so equal-power-per-octave input over 100 Hz-10 kHz keeps its power (computed from the sections' analytic responses at construction), and divides the gain by sqrt(width) (power follows bandwidth): a 110 Hz saw through every voice and vowel comes out -4.7 to +1.8 dB from its input RMS; `normalize: false` plays the table's levels (F1 at 0 dB, much quieter).  One kernel call per buffer, `FastFilter.formant` (in `fast_filter`, sharing `mb_svf.h`; exact Ruby mirror `Formant.process_ruby`): settings are recomputed only on samples where morph, shift, or width change (15 exp + 5 tan per change); same output at any buffer size; a plan boundary.  Cost over its input: constant settings ~20-27 ns/sample (0.1% of realtime at 128/800-sample buffers), every setting moving per sample ~160-170 ns/sample (0.8%, the worst case).  Before the kernel, five `Filter::SVF#dynamic_process` calls per buffer plus Numo morph math cost 220 ns/sample constant and ~900 with a moving morph at 128-sample buffers.

Musical vs hardware (a naming convention, maintainer 2026-10-17: "change :edge to :musical or a similar word that we could use anywhere I have chosen to change the traditional behavior of a source of inspiration"): wherever mb-sound departs from the hardware or design that inspired it, the playable version is `:musical` (the default) and the faithful one is `:hardware` (opt-in), under the option that names the behavior (here `tuning:`).  Its first use is lp4's and the diode ladder's cutoff tuning below (`tuning: :musical` / `tuning: :hardware`).  New departures use the same pair of names; existing options that are departures of this kind (e.g. the diode's `normalize:`) keep their names until they are revisited.

Cutoff tuning (filters-1, 2026-10-17; maintainer, asked whether lp4's frequency should move with resonance: "the goal for me is not fidelity to hardware, it's playability and consistency").  `tuning: :musical` (the default for lp4, every tap, and the diode ladder; `Filter::FourPole::Tuning`) makes the cutoff mean the same at every resonance: without resonance lowpasses (lp4, lp2, diode) are 6 dB down at the cutoff, highpasses (hp2, hp4) 6 dB down from their top, bandpasses (bp2, bp4) peak there; once the resonant peak stands 6 dB over the passband (`:db` curve: lp4 from resonance 0.29, lp2 0.20, hp2 0.48, hp4 0.63, diode 0.41; bandpasses always) the peak is on the cutoff (measured within 0.1 cent at 100 Hz-12 kHz); in between the target moves from the edge onto the peak, weight smoothstep(peak dB / 6), linear in frequency (lowpasses follow their own -6 dB edge as it rises with resonance; highpasses keep their no-resonance edge); from the oscillation edge up the scale is 1, so self-oscillation is at the cutoff and bit-identical to the hardware tuning (`:stages` drive at resonance 1 self-oscillates 75 cents flat in both, as before).  Consequences: during a resonance sweep the peak never passes the cutoff and the -6 dB edge never falls below it (spec'd); lp4 without resonance is about 0.64 octave brighter than before at the same number (-3 dB at 0.676 × the cutoff, was 0.435), the diode about 1.05 octaves (0.588, was 0.284; its slope is gentle: 6, 8, 11, 16 dB/octave over the first four octaves from the cutoff, lp4's 11, 18, 22, 24); lp2 without resonance is unchanged (its -6 dB point already sat at the stages' frequency).  `tuning: :hardware` is the old mapping (the stages' frequency at the cutoff; the diode's `normalize:` then also matches the cutoff to hardware lp4 as round 3 did; bit-identical to master-ai, checked by digest over every mode, curve, and drive mode).  With the musical tuning the diode's `normalize:` matches only levels (compensation, headroom).

-6 dB or -3 dB (Bench set filters-1-2026-10-17, maintainer's verdicts): the first version (then named `:edge`) put the -3 dB point at the cutoff without resonance and an experiment (`:edge6`) the -6 dB point, otherwise identical (the same peak placement from the 6 dB peak up).  -6 dB won every cutoff sweep (lp4 at resonance 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.6; the diode at 0 and 0.6), so it became the one musical tuning and the -3 dB variant was removed (superseded options are not kept; `:edge` never merged, so it has no alias); its tables were the same as today's but for the edge level, and the 128-segment tables come from it (edge6's sharper blend needed 128 where -3 dB had 64).  The resonance sweeps were picked "after" (the -3 dB version) over the hardware tuning; the patches (sq80, dnb_reese, acid) were marked the same.

Implementation: the kernels multiply the prewarped cutoff by a scale m(x), x = loop gain over the oscillation edge's (k / 4, the diode's k / 901/49), computed only when the resonance changes (`fp_tuning_scale` in `mb_four_pole.h`, exact mirror `Tuning.scale`): a cubic Hermite interpolation of generated tables (`mb_four_pole_tuning.h`, `four_pole/tuning_tables.rb`; one per tap and the diode, 128 segments over x 0..1, values and slopes; `lib/mb/sound/filter/four_pole/make_tuning.rb [--check]`, ~12 s) sampled from the definition in `FourPole::TuningAnalysis` (analog responses of each tap, numeric peak and edge searches; specs and the generator only).  Interpolation error under 0.5 cent everywhere (worst 0.4 cent, hp4 in its blend region; lp4 0.06).  Scaling g (not the cutoff before the tan) keeps the bilinear mapping exact at the cutoff at any frequency.  Kernel arguments: `four_pole(..., clip, tuning)` 0 hardware or 1..6 (the tap's table: lp4, lp2, bp2, bp4, hp2, hp4), `diode_ladder(..., normalize, tuning)` 0 or 7 (`fp_tuning_valid`); plans pass the tuning as a 14th setting.  Alternatives considered: a fitted rational m(k) like `diode_cutoff_scale` (the target is non-monotonic in k for lowpasses, which low-order rationals fit poorly, and there are seven targets); exact per-change solves (lp4's peak is a closed-form cubic, (1 + u)³ = k (3 - u) with u = w², and its edge a quartic, but the other taps and the diode have no handy closed forms, and Ruby's `Math.cbrt` adds a Newton step on glibc, so an exact mirror would need its own); a pure edge definition at every resonance (the peak would sit well below the cutoff at full resonance and self-oscillation off the cutoff); the dominant pole's frequency at the cutoff (needs the same blend at low resonance, and the pole's frequency is not where the response peaks at moderate resonance); "-3 dB below the peak on its upper side" (continuous, but the pitch drifts 100+ cents over r 0.3-1).

`quality:` with the musical tuning gives the gain q at the cutoff, the SVF's and cookbook's meaning: 0.5 or less is no resonance (the gain at the cutoff without resonance); lp4 q 0.707 → r 0.18, 1 → 0.21, 2 → 0.29, 4 → 0.44, 10 → 0.63, 70+ → 1; diode 0.31, 0.34, 0.41, 0.54, 0.69, 73+ → 1 (`FourPole.musical_quality_table`, the analog response's gain at the cutoff inverted in log q; each filter its own, so a diode at the same Q has the same gain at the cutoff).  `tuning: :hardware` keeps log(4q)/log(196).  The SVF and cookbook filters are unchanged (recommendation: keep the cookbook's standard meaning): their gain at the cutoff is Q (Q 0.707 is -3 dB there, Q 0.5 -6 dB), and their peak sits below the cutoff at moderate Q (Q 1: 0.707 × the cutoff, +1.25 dB; Q 1.5: -218 cents; Q 2: -116 cents, +6.3 dB; Q 4: -27; Q 10: -4), where lp4's is on it.

The r 0.9 cutoff sweep on the Bench (`cut_lp4_r9_*`, "Sample clipped and didn't sweep back like the title says"): both were the render, not the filter.  It didn't sweep back because `tween([100.hz, 5000.hz, 100.hz], 4.bars)` takes 4 bars per keyframe (`step` is each value's length), so the 4-bar render ends on the way up (4785 Hz at the last buffer); `tween([100.hz, 5000.hz], 2.bars)` (looping back) or 2-bar steps would have made the round trip in 4 bars (every `cut_*` job had this).  It clipped because a full-scale 110 Hz saw with the +32 dB resonant peak of resonance 0.9 on its fundamental and low harmonics (the cutoff near 100-300 Hz for the first 3 s) peaks at +10 dBFS after the -10 dB master gain (+4.5 dB at 2-3 s; hardware and musical tunings alike; resonance 0.6 reaches -0.8 dBFS), which the integer FLAC clipped before `loudness: -18` scaled it down, keeping the flat tops (the file's peak sat at exactly -9.9 dBFS from 0.5 to 3 s).  The filter behaves as designed (resonance 1 is +36.9 dB at the peak; the SQ-80-like compensation keeps the bass, not the peak, in check).  The library gap was that renders clipped silently (stems already warned): `render` now warns when a file's mix goes over full scale, with the `gain:` that leaves headroom (`MB::Sound::RenderPeak`, 2026-10-17).

Cost (ns/sample, 512-sample buffers, drive 1.5 :input, alternated runs against a master-ai build): constant parameters unchanged (lp4 37, diode 39-43); per-sample resonance lp4 61 → 97 (the scale, then g/(1+g), G⁴, and 1/(1 + k G⁴) every change, a serial chain), :stages 100 → 137; diode 112 → 112-118 (it already recomputed its coefficients per change); cutoff and resonance both per sample lp4 72 → 99, diode 122-130 → 126-135.  `tuning: :hardware` costs what master-ai did.  Renders: `/app/tmp/listening/filters-1/` (bench set filters-1-2026-10-17: resonance and cutoff sweeps, acid, sq80 :classic/:glass, :dnb_reese, before/after at -18 LUFS).

Denormal flushing (fixes-7, 2026-10-13, maintainer: "on the 800 grid instead of per buffer"): the SVF (`FastFilter.svf`, the plan's SVF and lane-major SVF, loop SVFs in `fast_loop`), the four-pole and diode ladder (`mb_four_pole_run`/`mb_diode_ladder_run`, the plan's and the lane-major kernels), and the formant filter flush states below 1e-30 (or not finite) to zero right after every 800th sample of their own time (`MB_FLUSH_GRID`/`mb_flush_value`/`mb_flush_count_read` in `mb_svf.h`, `mb_fp_flush_tick`; mirrors `Filter::SVF::FLUSH_GRID`, `.flush_count`, `.flush_value`), counting samples in an extra state entry (SVF `[ic1, ic2, count]`, four-pole `[s0..s3, count]`, formant's DFloat entry 10; states without it start counting at 0).  Before, they flushed at the end of each buffer (loop SVFs after every sample), so tails below 1e-30 differed by buffer size (a persona take's float stems, -590 dB).  For a run of 800-sample buffers starting with the filter it flushes where it did before.  Cost: SVF 15.7 -> 16.7 ns/sample at 800-sample buffers, four-pole, diode, and formant within noise.

## Later

- A filter-bank effect (parallel bands as one node: vocoder-style splits, graphic EQs, resonant banks) replaces `Filter::FilterBank` (the maintainer's 2020 class for parallel filters over Arrays of plain data, not used in graphs; maintainer 2026-10-30: "can be removed if we add a replacement to a future effects round"), which stays until then.
- Removed (omnibus18, 2026-10-30; maintainer: "Remove gain for sure"): `Filter::Gain`, a constant-gain Filter superseded by `node * gain` (Multiplier) and `GraphNode#at`.  `Filter::SimpleEnvelopeFollower` moved to the dynamics code as `GraphNode::Dynamics::SimpleEnvelopeFollower` (dynamics.md, Later).
