# Granular synthesis and spectral time-stretching

<!-- summary -->
`sample.grains(density:, size:, position:, scan:, pitch:, ...)` (aliases `granular`, `cloud`; `GraphNode::Grains`, a stereo MultiOutput, a plan boundary) plays a cloud of windowed reads of a Sample's band-limited levels, every option a number, Length, knob, or node read per grain onset; `v.grains(sample, ...)` key-tracks in synth voices.  Kernel `FastSampler.grains` (exact mirror `Granular::KernelRuby`): exact-sample onsets, six splitmix64 draws each, fixed slots.  `Sample#stretch(r, mode:)` offers `:cyclic` (S950), `:spectral` (phase vocoder, identity phase locking, kept transients; loops stretched as an endless stream, `wrap:`), `:paul` (Paulstretch), and `:slice` (loops.md), `randomize:` between vocoder and Paulstretch phases, `lock: :shape` keeping a tone's waveform (loops' default; `lock: :peaks` opts out); live followers restretch on workers.  Help `help :granular`.
<!-- /summary -->

## Quick reference

- `sample.grains(density:, size:, position:, scan:, pitch:)` (aliases `granular`, `cloud`; a stereo bundle), `v.grains(sample, ...)` in voices; `sample.stretch(r, mode: :cyclic | :spectral | :paul | :slice)`.
- Spectral options: `wrap:`, `noise:`, `randomize:`, `transients:`, `lock: :shape | :peaks` (waveform kept; looped stretches default to `:shape` by `Spectral.loop_lock`, unlooped to `:peaks`), `closure: :spread | :shift`, `closure_length:` (how a loop's phases close); `Spectral.looped(report:)` gives `:closure` (and `:shift`); `Spectral.shape_shift` (FFT) / `shape_shift_exhaustive` (its reference).
- Live followers: `follow(stretch: { mode: :spectral, interim: :slice | :hold | :coarse })`; `LoopPlayer::Restretch` (`.background?`, `.stats`, `.simulate = { interim:, full: }` for renders as if live); spec `spec/lib/mb/sound/graph_node/loop_player_restretch_spec.rb`; Bench set `stretch-live-1-2026-11-22`.
- Kernel `FastSampler.grains` (mirror `Granular::KernelRuby`); spec `spec/lib/mb/sound/granular_spec.rb`; demo `bin/songs/granular_song.rb`; help `help :granular`.

Branch `granular-1` (2026-10-14).  The maintainer picked "Granular + spectral stretch" from the Did You Know notes "Granular synthesis" and "The four families of time-stretching" (both "note for later" until then).  Builds on the sampler (`sampler.md`: `Sample`, its band-limited playback levels, `fast_sampler`) and the cyclic `Sample#stretch` (`fit: :auto`).

## API

```ruby
bowl = Sample['bowl.flac']
bg :drone, bowl.grains(position: 0.42, size: 120.ms, density: 30, spread: 0.02, pan_spread: 0.8)   # frozen
bg :rain,  rain.grains(scan: 0.25, size: 60.ms, density: 80, pitch_spread: 3, reverse: 0.3)         # a 4x slower cloud
bg :scrub, pad.grains(position: tween([0, 1], 8.bars), size: 80.ms)                                  # scrubbing
bg :shimmer, pad.grains(pitch: [0, 12, 19], pitch_spread: 0.1, window: :expodec)                    # pitch spray
synth(midi) { |v| v.grains(pad, density: 40, size: 70.ms) * v.env(0.2, 0.3, 0.8, 1.2) }             # key-tracked voices
pad.grains(read: :drop)                                                                               # lofi drop-sample reads

pad.stretch(8, mode: :spectral)        # phase vocoder, identity phase locking, hits on the stretched beat
pad.stretch(8, mode: :spectral, randomize: 1)     # Paulstretch-like phases, the vocoder's 4096-sample window (0..1 in between)
pad.stretch(32, mode: :paul)           # Paulstretch: big windows, random phases (seeded)
voice.stretch(8, mode: :paul, overlap: 2, shape: :paul, link: false)  # Nasca Paul's original settings (the voice pick)
pad.stretch(2)                         # the cyclic S950 stretch (mode: :cyclic, fit: :auto) stays the default
```

## Granular node (`GraphNode::Grains`)

- Signature: `sample.grains(density:, size:, position:, scan:, spread:, pitch:, pitch_spread:, pan_spread:, reverse:, window:, jitter:, seed:, read:, max_grains:)` (aliases `granular`, `cloud`); in voices `v.grains(sample, ...)`.
- `Sample#grains(**options)` returns a stereo `Channels` bundle of a `GraphNode::Grains` (MultiOutput, two outputs; alternates two sets of output buffers like ChannelMixer, so a Tee may share the last frame's).  `Notes#grains(sample, **options)` (`v.grains`) adds the voice's `freq:` (key tracking: each grain's speed times freq / the sample's root frequency) and `trigger:` (a note-on restarts the scan playhead and starts a grain on its exact sample).
- Every parameter is a number, Length/Interval where it fits, Knob, or node, read per sample; a grain reads the values on its onset sample and keeps them (the classic per-grain parameters):
  - `density:` grains per second (20; a Pitch or a node of Hz; a Duration (`1.n16`) is tempo-synced through its TempoNode frequency).
  - `jitter:` onset randomness, 0 (synchronous: a grain every 1/density, which buzzes at the density) to 1 (default: Poisson onsets, Roads' asynchronous cloud): each interval is 1/density × ((1 - j) + j × -ln(1 - u)), mean 1/density at any jitter.
  - `size:` grain length (100 ms; seconds, a Length, or a node of seconds), 2 samples to 10 s.
  - `position:` the grain center as a 0..1 fraction of the sample region in play order (a Length is converted; a node scrubs).  `position: 0.42` freezes.
  - `scan:` the playhead's speed relative to the sample's own (1 plays through in real time, 0.25 is a 4x granular stretch; a node), added to `position` and wrapping around the region.  Default: 1 when no `position:` is given (plain `rain.grains` plays the sample through, looping), else 0.
  - `spread:` ± random position offset (0..1 of the region, or a Length), clamped to the region.
  - `pitch:` semitones (an Interval, number, or node), or an Array of semitones to pick from at random per grain (`[0, 12, 19]`: octave and fifth spray); `pitch_spread:` ± random semitones.
  - `pan_spread:` 0 (center) to 1 (each grain anywhere from hard left to hard right): equal-power pans × sqrt(2) (center is unity on both sides, like Unison spread).  A stereo sample's grains read both channels and the pan acts as a balance.
  - `reverse:` the chance a grain plays backwards (0..1; true = 1).
  - `window:` `:hann` (default), `:triangle`, `:tukey` (half flat), `:gauss`, `:blackman`, `:rect` (64-sample edges), `:expodec` (sharp attack, exponential decay: Roads' percussive grains), `:rexpodec` (reversed); or a Proc of 0..1, or an Array of values.  Tabulated in Ruby (1024 + 1 points, linear interpolation, the same table in C and the mirror).
  - `normalize:` (true = `:power`) each grain's gain is 1 / sqrt(max(1, density × size × mean(window²))), so dense clouds of uncorrelated grains keep the sample's level and sparse grains play at full level.  Grains that line up into the original sound (scan 1, pitch 0, no spread: every grain reads the same source sample at each output sample) add up coherently instead (+11 dB at 400/s × 50 ms; measured): `normalize: :amplitude` divides by the windows' overlap (density × size × mean(window)) for that case; `false` turns it off.
  - `max_grains:` (32) the most grains sounding at once: an onset with no free slot is skipped (no stealing, no clicks), so the worst-case cost per sample is fixed.
  - `seed:` per-grain randomness from one splitmix64 state (`wt_noise_random`, mirror `Tone.noise_random`) seeded from `seed:` or `MB::Sound.next_seed`; every onset draws exactly six numbers (position, pitch choice, pitch jitter, pan, reverse, next interval) whatever the settings, so changing one parameter doesn't reshuffle the others.
  - `read:` `:clean`/`:drop` (or `interpolation:`/`mips:`) as for `Sample#read_as`; default the sample's own read (the lofi presets read drop-sample).
- Grain geometry: a grain of L output samples at speed s (2^(semitones/12) × source rate / output rate × key ratio) reads positions a + k·s (backwards: from a + (L-1)·s down to a); its center goes at the position and its start a is clamped so every read lies inside the region; a grain longer than the region is shortened to fit (never cut mid-window).  The window is read at k × 1024 / L.
- Kernel `FastSampler.grains` (`ext/mb/sound/fast_sampler/granular.c`, sharing fast_sampler's `-ffp-contract=off` build and `mb_wavetable.h`): onsets on exact samples from a per-sample accumulator (so the output is the same at any buffer size); each active grain is one `wt_value_dispatch` (two levels of the sample's band-limited table, selected by |s|) per channel, a window lookup, and two multiply-adds.  Exact Ruby mirror `Granular::KernelRuby.grains` (specs compare sample for sample).  State: a DFloat row per grain slot and a few globals (onset phase, interval scale, playhead), plus the UInt64 RNG.
- A plan boundary (`plan_unsupported_reason`).  Never ends (like oscillators) unless an input ends.
- Live audio: grains play Samples, so `capture_audio(4.bars)` (once done, `cap.sample`) and `bounce` results work.  A Clouds-style rolling live buffer (grains reading a ring that a node writes) is left for later: it needs a non-mipmapped read path.

## Spectral and Paulstretch modes (`Sample::Spectral`)

Offline edits like the cyclic stretch (baked into a new Sample, cached per audio and arguments; `Sample::EDITS`).  `Sample#stretch(ratio, mode: :cyclic | :spectral | :paul, ...)` (short: `Sample#stretch(r, mode:)`; `:cyclic` is the default, the S950 stretch; `:slice` stretches by slices, with its own options `slices:`, `fill:`, `fade:`, `slot_fade:`, `fade_law:`, `positions:`, `loops.md`); `:pv`/`:vocoder` alias `:spectral`, `:paulstretch` aliases `:paul`.  `:spectral` is `Sample::Spectral.vocoder` (about 25-50x realtime per output second, below).  Results over 30 s read with `mips: :octave` (round 2).  Help `help :granular`; demo `bin/songs/granular_song.rb`.

- `:spectral`: a phase vocoder (Hann windows of `window:` 4096 samples, `overlap:` 4, i.e. a fixed synthesis hop N/4 and analysis hops N/4 / ratio; FFTs through Numo::Pocketfft on copies) with Laroche & Dolson (1999) identity phase locking: peaks are bins above their two neighbours on each side; each peak's phase advances by its instantaneous frequency over the synthesis hop; every other bin keeps its analysis phase offset from the peak whose region it is in (regions split midway between peaks).  Channels are locked together: the phase rotation is computed on the channels' mix and applied to every channel, so the stereo image doesn't wander.  Transients (`transients: true`): each onset from `Sample::Onsets` lands at ratio × its input time, with an unstretched span (a window and a hop before it, half a window after: every frame holding the attack) and the input between spans stretched to fill the time between them; frames holding an onset reset the phases of bins rising 6 dB (`RESET_RISE`) to their analysis phases (granular-2; see below).  Output length round(ratio × length), aligned with the input's start (frames padded by half a window).
- `:paul`: Paulstretch (Nasca Paul): windows of `window:` 0.25 s, synthesis hop N / `overlap:` (4), analysis hop that / ratio, magnitudes kept and every phase replaced by a random one.  Random phases come from a counter-based splitmix64 hash of (seed, frame, bin) computed with Numo (vectorized, the same at any chunking), `seed:` 0 by default (stretching the same sample the same way gives the same audio, and the edit cache stays meaningful); all channels share the phases (the image keeps its level panning).  For extreme ratios (8x-1000x+).
- Realtime: both could run streaming (a frame per hop, latency one window), but they are offline for now (bounce-time edits, no per-voice CPU).

## As built and measured (granular-1, 2026-10-14)

- Onsets snap: an accumulated phase within 1e-9 of 1 starts a grain on that sample (100 grains per second at 48 kHz landed every 481st sample from rounding before).
- Reading one output alone (`grains[0]`) is fine; `.mono`/`.mixdown` for a mono cloud (the left output alone loses right-panned grains).
- Grain cost (AMD 7950X, min of 3 runs, `/tmp`-style bench of a 3 s noise sample; ns per output sample, both outputs): defaults (20/s × 100 ms, ~2 active) 105 / 59 ns at 128 / 800-sample buffers (0.50% / 0.29% of realtime); worst case, all 32 slots sounding: mono 624 / 583 ns (3.0% / 2.8%), stereo 1191 / 1137 ns (5.7% / 5.5%), stereo with node inputs 1308 / 1201 ns (6.3% / 5.8%), drop-sample reads 289 / 251 ns (1.4% / 1.2%), `:sinc` 5715 / 5883 ns (27-28%).  About 18 ns per grain per channel with `:optimal` reads.  So a synth with 8 stereo grain voices costs up to ~45% of realtime at the default cap; lower `max_grains:` for voices.
- Spectral stretch speed (2 s stereo chord): 4x 0.27 s (30x realtime of output), 32x 1.3 s (50x); Paulstretch 8x 0.17-0.23 s.  Instantaneous frequencies come from a second FFT N/8 before each frame (so quality doesn't depend on the ratio, and huge ratios with zero analysis hops work), except in unstretched (transient) frames, which use the real hop so they rebuild the input exactly.
- Transients: a noise burst stretched 4x rises (5% → 50% of its peak) in 2-6 samples with `transients: true`, ~2500 samples without.
- `transients:` with positions (acid-loop-info-1, 2026-11-10): `:markers` (the sample's markers, a loop file's stretch markers when it has them; detected onsets when it has none) or a list of positions as `slices:` takes them drive the same handling instead of `Sample::Onsets` (`Spectral.vocoder`'s `onsets:`); `false` or an empty list is the plain vocoder.  A position with no attack in the audio still gets its unstretched span and lands on its stretched time, the stretch between positions making up for it (a grid of markers on a held pad stretches unevenly: one for the listening study, `loops.md`).  `confirm: true` (or a reach) keeps only the positions with a detected onset within 15 ms, and the start, each on its onset (`Sample#confirmed_onsets`; stretch-fixes-1, `loops.md`).
- Paulstretch frames are random-phase, so they add in power: the overlap-add divides by sqrt(Σw² × mean(w²)) instead of Σw² (the plain normalization played 6 dB low).

## Round 2 (maintainer answers, 2026-10-14)

- Clouds keep 32 slots; `v.grains` (Notes#grains) defaults to `Granular::VOICE_MAX_GRAINS` = 16 per voice (`max_grains:` still overrides), so 8 stereo grain voices cost at most ~23% of realtime instead of ~45%.
- Stretch results longer than `Sample::LONG_STRETCH` (30 s) read with `mips: :octave` (about 8x the audio for levels instead of about 14x) unless the source sample has its own mips (an explicit `read_as(mips:)`, a drop read); voices' and grains' `mips:` options still win.
- Later follow-ups, not now: the Clouds-style live grain buffer, and an opt-in grain layer for the ambient genre.

## Round 3 (granular-2, 2026-10-15)

From the maintainer's Bench verdicts on granular-1 (picks: pad 2x/4x spectral, 8x/32x paul; voice 2x spectral, 4x/8x/32x paul; bass 2x/4x/32x spectral; break 32x paul).  Scratch measurement scripts were in /tmp/granular-2 (clicks/s: 2nd differences over 8× their 10 ms RMS, counted once per 5 ms; YIN pitch; onset timing).

- **Cyclic clicks ("not windowed (or rect window)?")**: yes: the default `crossfade:` was 0, so every splice was a hard cut, and `fit: :auto` snapped each new cycle's start to the nearest rising zero crossing after the correlation match, undoing it by up to 24 samples.  Now `crossfade: 3.ms` (`Sample::CROSSFADE`): raised-cosine fades divided by sqrt(a² + b² + 2ρab), ρ the two sides' correlation (`Lofi.crossfade_gains`): equal gain where the splice matches, equal power where it doesn't; splices too near the end fade in early (`Lofi.preroll`); `crossfade: 0` keeps hard cuts (ends at zero crossings with :auto).  Clicks/s granular-1 → now: bass 8x 36.8 → 0, pad 8x 18.5 → 0.6, break 2x/4x/8x 16.8/35.0/43.8 → 0.3/1.5/1.2 (sources 0-0.7; a break's repeated hits count).
- **Bass pitch jumps**: the period search only looked within ±25% of the cycle (900..1500 samples at 25 ms), where a 55 Hz period (872.7) and its multiples aren't: 645 of 656 cycles were 900 samples (1.03 periods) and some the asked 1200; and the splice search (±300) was narrower than a period, so a third of the splices had no matching phase in reach (sidebands at 55 ± 7 Hz).  Now `Lofi.local_period`: the shortest normalized-autocorrelation peak after the first dip below zero (within 0.1 of the best, ≥ FIT_PERIOD_MIN, lags up to 1.25 cycles, parabola-refined), followed from cycle to cycle (a peak within 6% of the last period wins) and held FIT_PERIOD_HOLD cycles when lost; cycles are round(cycle / period) whole periods; the splice search reaches ±0.55 period, and near-ties (`MATCH_TIE`) pick among correlation peaks nearest the clock.  Bass 8x: 543/545 YIN frames voiced, all within 25 cents of 55 Hz (granular-1: 320 voiced, up to 1024 cents off).
- **Spectral breaks ("the first hit holds, then the rest lunges forward")**: the onsets' unstretched spans (5120 samples) were as long as a 16th at 140 BPM, so they merged into one run played at ratio 1 and the bits between took all the stretch (4x: the 2nd hit 470 ms late, 8x 1.1 s).  Now anchored spans (`Spectral.time_map`): every kept onset lands at ratio × its time, spans shrink to fit with MIN_GAP hops between them.  The full phase reset at onsets then dipped the ringing kick before each hit (-13 dB at 4x: frames before and after the reset overlap-add with unrelated phases); resetting only rising bins measured -4.4 dB, the input's own -4.3.  Onset timing on the break: within 0.1 ms at 2x/4x/8x.  Smaller windows (1024) measured worse on this break (16-72 extra onsets from phasey tails), so no separate drum mode.
- **One algorithm, `randomize:`**: 0..1 adds up to ± randomize × π of random phase per bin per frame after locking (0 the vocoder, :spectral's default; 1 Paulstretch, :paul's default, which runs `Spectral.paul`'s magnitude-only frames when no onsets are kept: bit-identical to granular-1).  Each frame's coherence c = sinc(randomize) is summed with the windows and `finish` divides by sqrt(Σcw² ² + (Σw² - Σc²w²) mean(w²)); level change over randomize 0..1 within 0.35 dB (chord) and 1.2 dB (noise).  Frames holding an attack stay unrandomized, so `:paul, transients: true` (default false) keeps attacks: a noise burst at 4x rises in 2 samples (9873 without), at the input level.
- **Paulstretch vs the original** (paulstretch_python `paulstretch_stereo.py`, paulstretch_cpp `Stretch.cpp`): both randomize every phase uniformly every frame, as we do.  Differences: overlap 2 (hop half a window), the Python window (1 - x²)^1.25 (C++: rectangular/Hamming/Hann/Blackman/Blackman-Harris), per-channel phases (separate generators), window 0.25 s as ours; the C++ app also has onset sensitivity (snap to an onset, then pay the time back) and spectral processing (harmonics, spread, pitch/frequency shift, filters).  The chorus, measured on a 440 Hz sine at 8x (power spectra averaged over 10 seeds, -6 dB width of the line): Hann overlap 4 (ours) 10.25 Hz, Hann overlap 2 10.25, (1 - x²)^1.25 overlap 4 9.52, overlap 2 6.59, Python original 6.59.  New options: `overlap:` (used by :paul now), `shape: :hann | :paul` (`Spectral::SHAPES`), `link: false` (per-channel random phases).
- **Verdicts (granular-2 Bench, 2026-10-15)**: Paulstretch defaults stay (the pad picked the current Hann/overlap 4/linked sound); the voice picked the original-like recipe `stretch(8, mode: :paul, overlap: 2, shape: :paul, link: false)` ("all useful"), kept as the documented recipe for voices; `randomize:` picked 1 in the vocoder's 4096-sample window (`mode: :spectral, randomize: 1`), the help example; breaks picked the new spectral and Paulstretch-with-transients results at 2x/4x/8x (no slice-style drum stretch for now); the 3 ms adaptive crossfade stays the default for `fit: :fixed` too (`crossfade: 0` is the raw splice).  Song round 3: the rain's hall 12 dB lower, and the echo's last note (the resolving D) carried through the outro instead of stopped at bar 17.
- **Song**: the sources were bounced at 120 BPM (`bpm 72` in the song queues a Session tempo change; `Sequence.transport.bpm` still read 120): `granular_song_sources(bpm:)`.  (fixes-14 made the library read the render's tempo, `Sequence.current_transport`; the spray's `density: 1.n16` had also been read at 120, so the song now says `density: 8`, the 8 grains a second of the Bench pick, until an A/B against 1.n16 at 72 is decided.)  The phrase is A C E D, the spray moves Dm7 Gm7 Am7 Dm7, the close returns to drone and rain with the drone moving G → D; the rain has a ping-pong delay and a hall.

## Loops: the seam (stretch-fixes-1, 2026-11-13; waits for the maintainer's ears)

The maintainer on the stretch study's Bench set (a sustained pad loop at 0.77x, the phase vocoder; verdict "source, unstretched"): "The vocoder is picking up false transients and making a clicking sound".  Measured on the clips he heard: one click per pass of the loop, at the wrap (1.599 s apart; the second difference there 8.5 times its 10 ms RMS with the vocoder's own onsets, 11.3 times with a marker at every grid position), none inside a pass, none in the source or the slice stretch.  The detector reports one onset on that pad, position 0 (its "a sound that starts loud" rule), and that one is not what clicks: it makes the output start as the input does (first frame with the input's phases, then the unstretched span).  The end of the stretch arrives with whatever phases it has reached, so the last sample steps to the first: 0.0056 at 0.77x and 0.017 at 1.25x where the input's own wrap steps 0.0003 (without transients 0.027).

- Round 1 (c2d56b17) crossfaded the output's last hop (21 ms) into the input's own last hop, a pre-roll into the loop start: the step went (0.0056 → 0.0003) and so did the click, but the maintainer still heard it (Bench, 2026-11-14): "The unstretched source loops smoothly.  The phase vocoder still introduces a tonal discontinuity on the loop boundary", and "I'm confident that if the original sample were repeated several times first, and then the result stretched, these blips/discontinuities in tone would not be present in the output."  He was right, and it shows in the spectral flux (the change between magnitude spectra of 2048-sample frames 1024 samples apart, as a share of their sum, at the wrap against inside a pass; three tiled passes of the pad):

  | pad at 0.77x / 1.25x | step at the wrap | flux at the wrap | inside: median, 99%, max |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | source | 0.0003 | 0.179 | 0.135, 0.208, 0.236 |
  | the stretch's own end | 0.0056 / 0.0172 | 0.438 / 0.352 | 0.147, 0.227, 0.233 / 0.118, 0.191, 0.221 |
  | 21 ms pre-roll (round 1) | 0.0003 / 0.0003 | 0.394 / 0.282 | (the same) |
  | read in a circle, phases closed (now) | 0.0001 / 0.0001 | 0.173 / 0.161 | (the same) |
  | three passes stretched as one, the whole | 0.0001 / 0.0005 | 0.175 / 0.158 | (the same) |
  | three passes, the middle one looped alone | 0.085 / 0.096 | 0.532 / 0.525 | (the same) |

  The pre-roll's wrap changed tone 2.4 to 2.7 times as much as the middle of a pass (above anything inside a pass): about 100 ms of unstretched sound, and two phase turns, in a pad that is vocoder sound everywhere else.
- Now (`Spectral.looped`, what `Spectral.vocoder(wrap: true)` runs; one `Spectral::Vocoder` makes the frames for all forms): the loop is read as an endless stream (every frame wraps around the ends: no frame sees an edge or a zero), the time map is the middle of three passes (an onset's unstretched span reaches across the wrap), and the output is overlap-added in a circle.  A stretch's partials don't return to their starting phases by themselves (the middle pass of three, looped alone, is the worst row above), so a first pass measures each bin's phase error at the wrap and the second adds it, spread evenly over the frames, to every frame's phase advance before locking: each partial moves by less than half a cycle per loop (0.3 Hz for a 1.6 s loop) and ends where it started.  Closure error (magnitude-weighted mean, cycles) on the pad 0.32 → 0.028 at 0.77x, 0.24 → 0.043 at 1.25x; on a steady synthetic pad 0.19 → 0.000.  (Round 2 also offered `passes: n`, n passes stretched as one piece; he heard one pass, seven passes, and the live stream as equally fine ("Use whichever is best for CPU and playability"), so round 4 removed the option: the cached closed loop is the default (0.6 to 0.7% of a core while playing against the live stream's 6.5 to 7.7%, at 800-sample buffers on the Mac), and `live: true` stays opt-in.  `Spectral.looped(passes:)` remains for the measurement above.)
- `Sample::Onsets.detect(wrap: true)`: a loop that starts loud has an onset at 0 only if the envelope rises by the threshold across the loop point, so the pad has none (round 1 kept it as the anchor of the pre-roll) and is vocoder sound all the way round.
- `Sample#stretch(mode: :spectral | :paul, wrap:)`: `wrap: nil` (default) treats a sample that loops forward over its whole region (`Sample#whole_loop?`: a loop file, or `.looped`) as a loop, `true` any sample, `false` none (a stretch with its own start and end, as before stretch-fixes-1).  `LoopPlayer` (`follow`) asks for `wrap: true` for the spectral modes unless the stretch options say (`LoopPlayer.loop_stretch`).  Paulstretch loops go through the same circle (random phases need no closing), except a loop whose loop point is an attack (stretch-fixes-3, below).  A sound change for looping samples and for `follow`'s spectral stretches (phases differ throughout; the level and the attacks do not): `wrap: false` is the old sound for the A/B.
- Live (`Spectral::Stream`, `follow(stretch: { mode: :spectral, live: true })`, `Sample#spectral_stream`): the same Vocoder making frames as the loop plays, each frame read from wherever the player's loop positions have got to (frames a hop apart on the stream's own sample count, positions half a window ahead taken from the current speed, the same time map around onsets as offline), so it is his "repeated, then stretched" with no end at all: nothing to close, nothing restretched when the tempo moves (the cached stretches are per 1/4 BPM, a new one made on a worker thread live since stretch-live-1, on the render thread before), tempo ramps smooth, a seek restarts the phases.  One stream serves a sample's channels (one phase rotation for all, as offline).  Measured on the pad at 0.77x, four passes: flux at the wraps 0.175 (inside: median 0.147, max 0.250), no click; bit for bit the same at 128- and 800-sample buffers at a steady tempo; a ramp from 0.77x to 1.25x and a jump of a third of the loop without clicks.  CPU (Linux, pure Ruby and Numo, a stereo loop): 3.1% of one core at 800-sample buffers, 5.0% at 128; on the Mac container (stretch-fixes-3, 2026-11-14, three stereo loops of 1.5-8.2 s through `follow`) 6.5-7.7% at 800 and 7.4-8.4% at 128, against the cached loop's 0.6-0.7% and 2.1-2.3%, with 450-460 objects allocated a buffer at 800 and 194-195 at 128 (cached: 136).  It allocates Numo arrays every hop, so it stays opt-in until a kernel does the frames. ## Long ratios: the window's buzz (stretch-fixes-1 round 2, 2026-11-14; the default since round 4)

The maintainer on the stretch study's Bench set (a tonal drum loop with a shaker at 2x and 8x, a cymbal splash at 2.9x): "The phase vocoder gets too rhythmic/buzzy at these longer stretch ratios", "B and C both get rhythmic", "a less buzzy version of B", and, asked what "rhythmic" meant: "'rhythmic' was referring to the same thing as buzzing -- whatever period the vocoder uses becomes audible".

Measured: the period is the window's, not the hop's.  A whitened autocorrelation of the output (16384- or 32768-sample frames, each spectrum divided by its own smoothed level so that partials don't count; value at a lag re lag 0) has one peak, at the window length:

| self-similarity one window later | source | 2x | 3.82x | 8x |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| white noise, window 4096, overlap 4 | 0.006 | 0.002 | 0.051 | 0.137 to 0.196 |
| texture pad, with / without transients | 0.005 | 0.019 / 0.007 | | 0.215 / 0.199 |
| chord stab, with / without transients | | | 0.090 / 0.078 | 0.198 / 0.193 |
| texture pad at 8x: overlap 8; window 8192 (at 8192); window 2048 (at 2048) | | | | 0.221; 0.144; 0.216 |
| texture pad at 8x: `randomize: 0.3`, `0.6`; Paulstretch | | | | 0.175, 0.094; 0.000 |

- Nothing at the hop (0.03 at one hop later, as the source), nothing at hop × (1 - 1 / ratio) (where copies of one input instant land in successive frames), no line at the frame rate in the bands' amplitude modulation (1 to 3 dB over its neighbourhood, the source's 5).  More overlap changes nothing; another window moves the period with it.
- Why: every frame is a sum of bin-centre sinusoids, one period of a series on a grid of sample rate / window (11.7 Hz), under a window.  A partial's phases turn at its own frequency from frame to frame, so the frames add up to that partial.  What is not a partial (noise, breath, a shaker's grain) is held as "partials" at whatever each region's peak bin measured, which is near the bin centres, and at a long ratio the frames a window apart analyse almost the same input: they come out alike, and the output repeats once a window, 11.7 times a second for 4096 samples, with its harmonics: a pulse and a buzz at once.  It grows with the ratio because the frames grow more alike, and it does not need onsets (the same without transients).
- An earlier reading of "rhythmic" here (this note, a day before) blamed the time map's unstretched spans around onsets.  They are uneven (at 8x the spans hold half the tonal drum loop's input and the rest is stretched 12.75x) but they are not what he heard: the period is there without them.

The fix (`Sample#stretch(mode: :spectral, noise: 0..1)`, `Spectral::Vocoder#noisiness`; an option in round 2, the default at long ratios since round 4, below): noise kept noise.  Each bin belongs to its region's peak (as for the phase locking); a peak standing 16 dB or more above its surroundings (the mean log magnitude 24 bins either side) is a partial, 8 dB or less is noise (peaks of noise under a Hann window stand 3 to 8 dB up), in proportion between; every frame, a bin's phase gets a random offset of up to ± `noise` × its noisiness × π (from `seed:`; counter-based, the same for every channel with `link:`), and the gain that keeps its level when frames with unrelated phases overlap (they add in power: hop / window of the coherent power, so up to +6 dB at overlap 4).  Frames holding an attack are not randomized.  Partials keep their locked phases.

| `noise: 1` against the plain vocoder | self-similarity one window later | fine flatness (source) | attack step | level re source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| white noise at 8x | 0.196 → 0.007 | | | -1.45 → -0.04 dB |
| a chord with hiss 14 dB under it, 8x | 0.196 → 0.009 | (the 220 Hz partial's share of its band 0.9999 both) | | -0.10 → -0.03 dB |
| texture pad at 2x | 0.019 → 0.004 | 0.716 → 0.755 (0.823) | 2.5 dB both | -0.2 dB both |
| texture pad at 8x | 0.215 → 0.017 | 0.568 → 0.744 (0.823) | 4.1 dB both | -0.2 dB both |
| chord stab at 3.82x / 8x | 0.090 → 0.008 / 0.198 → 0.026 | 0.310 → 0.588 / 0.288 → 0.643 | | -0.6 → -0.5 dB |
| kick pattern at 4x | 0.051 → 0.001 | 0.644 → 0.648 (0.648) | 32.7 dB both | the same |
| generated drum loop at 1.25x | 0.008 → 0.003 | 0.749 → 0.776 (0.733) | 7.3 dB both | -0.1 → +0.2 dB |

`noise: 0.5` does less than half of it (the pad at 8x: 0.150): the offsets must reach all the way round to stop frames agreeing.  It costs about 40% more time (the pad at 8x: 2.3 s against 1.6 s).  It works in loops (`wrap:`) and live streams (`follow(stretch: { mode: :spectral, live: true, noise: 1 })`).

Also there, all options, no default changed:
- `randomize:` 0..1 (granular-2) turns every bin, partials too, towards Paulstretch; frames holding an attack stay unrandomized, so "a transient-aware D with a bit less randomization" is `stretch(8, mode: :spectral, randomize: 0.6)` (the tonal drum loop's attack steps 4.1 dB against the source's 4.3 and Paulstretch's -1.1; self-similarity 0.094).  `mode: :paul, transients: true` does it in Paulstretch's quarter-second window, which keeps hits only when they are further apart than that (a kick pattern at 4x: attack steps 32.7 dB, the source's; Paulstretch alone -1.0).
- `transients:` takes an amount from 0 to 1 (`Spectral.time_map`'s `span`: how much of each onset's surroundings stays at ratio 1; 1 is `true`, 0 is `false`), for a more even stretch between attacks: on the tonal drum loop with a shaker at 8x 0.5 leaves the rest at 9.4x instead of 12.75x.  A kick pattern keeps its attacks at 0.5 (32.1 dB) and loses them at 0.25 (9.5 dB).  It does not touch the buzz.

Round 4 (2026-11-14): he picked the vocoder with `noise: 1` on the tonal drum loop at 2x and 8x and on the chord stab at 8x ("Randomize sounds just choppy"), heard no difference on the stab at 3.82x, and had picked the plain vocoder on drums at 1.18x and 1.25x.  So `noise:` now follows the ratio by default (`Spectral.noise_for`): 0 up to 1.5x (`NOISE_FROM`), 1 from 2x (`NOISE_FULL`), a straight line between (1.75x: 0.5), 0 when speeding up.  Up to 1.5x the stretch is sample for sample what it was (checked on the drum loop at 1.18x, the generated loop and a bass loop at 1.25x); `noise: 0` is the off switch and `noise: 1` forces it; a live stream follows the ratio it is playing at.  Why a ramp and not a step: a tempo ride through 1.5x to 2x under `follow` then changes by degrees.  Still open: a C kernel for the frames if the live stream is to carry it (Ruby and Numo now).

## The shape lock as the loops' default (stretch-lock-default-1, 2026-11-21)

The maintainer, asked "Make `lock: :shape` the default for looped spectral stretches?", picked "Default now", accepting that other stretched loops change sound too.  `Spectral.looped` (and `Spectral.vocoder(wrap: true)`, `Sample#stretch(mode: :spectral)` of a looping sample or with `wrap: true`, and so `follow`'s cached loops) takes `lock: nil` and resolves it with `Spectral.loop_lock(randomize, closure)`: `:shape`, except `:peaks` with `randomize: 1` (Paulstretch: every phase random, so the lock only costs time; `:paul` renders as before) and with `closure: :shift` (the two never converged together; an explicit `lock: :shape, closure: :shift` still raises).  `lock: :peaks` opts out (identity locking alone: the previous loops bit for bit) and prints in the lineage; the default does not print, so saved edits that stretch loops replay with the shape lock.  Unlooped stretches keep `:peaks` (`lock: :shape` asks); the live stream (`follow(stretch: { live: true })`, `Spectral::Stream`) takes no `lock:` and never closes a loop, so it is unchanged.

Cost on the Mac container (i9-9880H, stereo loops, min of two alternating runs, `Sample#stretch(r, mode: :spectral)` on a looped Sample; `:peaks` → `:shape`): a 1.53 s drum break 0.23 → 0.67 s at 1.285x, 0.16 → 0.37 at 0.8x, 0.13 → 0.51 at 1.0625x; 3 s of low strings 0.36 → 1.37 / 0.19 → 0.76 / 0.26 → 1.07; a 3.69 s bass take 0.40 → 1.26 / 0.21 → 0.77 / 0.29 → 1.03 (stretch-fixes-6 measured 0.6 → 1.3 on Linux); 8.2 s of strings 0.87 → 2.98 / 0.58 → 1.83 / 0.78 → 2.52: 2.4-4.1x.  `Spectral.shape_lock` is three quarters of it (4.2 ms a frame: the coarse search scores up to 2049 time shifts against 48 peaks, a cosine each).  On the render thread: a `bpm` change makes each cached spectral follower restretch inside one buffer (`LoopPlayer#stretched`), and the buffer after the change took 0.27-0.42 s (3 s loop) and 0.69-0.71 s (8.2 s loop) with `:peaks`, 0.89-1.00 s and 2.10-2.19 s with the default, against 16.7 ms for an 800-frame buffer: the restretch was already a dropout and the default makes it about 3x longer; building a follower (`follow` calls `#prepare` on the console thread) 0.73 → 2.10 s for the 8.2 s loop.  Noise: hiss at 4x with `noise: 0` measures less self-similar with the lock (0.033 against above 0.05), so the window buzz `noise:` exists for is smaller there too.  Specs: `sample_spectral_spec.rb` (the defaults, the loop players at three buffer sizes) and the graph sweep's `lock: :peaks` follower.  Bench set `stretch-lock-default-1-2026-11-21`.

## Live tempo changes (stretch-live-1, 2026-11-22)

The maintainer, asked how a live tempo change should handle the shape lock's restretch cost: "How did ACID manage this?  Do 1+2, and consider rougher but cheaper time shift algorithms in time+freq domain when tempo shifting live" (1: restretch off the audio thread; 2: score the shape lock's time shifts with one FFT).

How ACID stretches loops, from the Linux session's .acd study (the private study notes of 2026-11-10-stretch-study, `2026-11-14-slice-timing/`, `2026-11-15-windows-results-3/acd-win3-takes.md`; oracle `Acd::SliceTiming.acid_stretch` on `research-acd-format-linux`; his stems and test projects, ACID 11):
- The method is stored per clip (every loop take of ACID 5+ projects carries it; the file's own setting arrives with it in 199 of 219 clips): "looping" for nearly all loops, "non-looping" for a few dozen sustained phrases (recorded vocals and guitar, pad layers), "pitch shift" and "sliced" rarely.
- Every method is time domain, per slice at the file's stretch markers, with no spectral step: inside a slice the samples are the file's own (a -310 dB null).  Faster, each slice starts at its marker's scaled place, plays at file speed, and from a third of its slot crossfades at equal power into the lead-in of the next marker (looping and non-looping null to digital silence there; looping cuts at every marker, sliced only at the pinned ones).  Slower, "looping segments" fills each slot with n + 1 overlapping passes of the segment, n = ceil((r - 1) / 0.65), parabola-law crossfades; "sliced" plays each slice once with 2 ms edges, then silence; "non-looping" slowed plays short runs from varying places (another engine, not modelled); "pitch shift" resamples.  On a distorted bass loop this keeps the waveform exactly, which is why it beat the vocoder there (acd-acid-highs-1).  The library has the rules: `stretch(mode: :slice, slot_fade: 2/3r, fade_law: :power)` faster, `fill: :repeat, fade_law: :parabola` slower.
- Live behavior is not measured: no study timed ACID's CPU or watched it through a tempo change.  The Linux session infers that the rule is a per-slice map of positions with no analysis per tempo, so it can run per buffer at any tempo, and his projects' tempo automation renders continuously; the bounce request for a loop across a tempo jump and a ramp (HANDOFF item 2b) has no result yet.

What was built:
- `Spectral.shape_shift`: the shape lock's peaks placed on a frequency grid of 16x the reach (`SHAPE_GRID` 16384 for 4096-sample windows), one complex FFT gives the score of every shift (a cross-correlation in the frequency domain), the `SHAPE_CANDIDATES` (8) best local maxima (the ends of the reach too) are scored exactly over +-`SHAPE_AROUND` (4) samples, the best climbed to on the exact score while it sits at an edge, then the same parabola.  `shape_shift_exhaustive` (the old search) is its reference.  Over 12298 frames of seven CC0 loops at 0.8x, 1.0625x, 1.285x, and 2x: the same shift in 98.6% of frames; the rest are ties a whole period apart (scores within 0.1%, where the old coarse grid stepped two samples for low sounds; the FFT's pick scores higher in most) and one frame 2% below.  Explicit `:shape` renders: 22 of 28 loop/ratio pairs bit for bit as before, a marimba within -40 to -53 dB, two (a hummed voice and 8.2 s of strings at 2x) diverging from their first tie (the vocoder carries its phases; the lock keeps the shape either way).  The search went from 2.4 to 0.8 ms a frame and whole shape-locked stretches 1.4-2.4x faster: 1.2-2.1x the plain vocoder instead of 2.4-4.6x (8.2 s of strings at 1.285x: 3.02 -> 1.60 s, `lock: :peaks` 0.86 s; Mac container, min of two alternating runs).
- `LoopPlayer::Restretch`: on a realtime Session's render thread (`Restretch.background?`), a follower's stretch for a new tempo goes to a worker (two lanes, one thread each: `:interim` for quick stretches, `:full`), which hands the GVL to the render thread (`UI::Yielding`) and leaves GC alone (holding majors off with `GCQuiet` kept the stretch's arrays young, and the minors they then needed stopped the render thread 8-14 ms in most changes at 128-frame buffers).  Meanwhile the player plays its interim (`follow(stretch: { mode: :spectral, interim: })`, `LoopPlayer::INTERIMS`): `:slice` (the default for spectral followers: a slice stretch at the new tempo with ACID's rules, `SLICE_FASTER`/`SLICE_SLOWER`), `:hold` (the stretch it was playing, read at the new speed by linear interpolation: the pitch moves with the tempo), or `:coarse` (the vocoder without the shape lock at overlap 2); followers whose own stretch is quick hold.  Whenever what plays changes it crossfades over `SWAP_FADE` (50 ms, raised cosine; both reads are at the same loop phase, so they line up).  Jobs for tempos left behind are cancelled before they start; finished ones are cached with the rest.  Offline renders, `process_buffer` callers, and `#prepare` make stretches at once, as before (same output at any buffer size); `Restretch.simulate = { interim:, full: }` acts as live in any render, deterministically (the Bench set's renders, the spec).
- Measured live (Mac container, stereo CC0 loops, NullOutput paced by the wall clock, the render thread's time per buffer without the output's wait; base fe5b63c2 against this branch): 120 -> 126 BPM at 800-frame buffers, 3 s cello loop 1189 ms -> 3.4 ms worst buffer, 8 s violin loop 2508 -> 2.2-7.2 ms (the full stretch taken 0.7-0.8 s and 1.4-1.9 s after the change, depending on the interim's competing work); a ramp to 128 over 2 bars fell 8.1 / 9.6 s behind the clock before, worst buffer 2.8-3.8 ms after (the full stretch landing 4.8-6.2 s after the ramp began, the slice interim restretched 29-59 times).  At 128-frame buffers (2.7 ms) what remains is garbage collection while a worker allocates: with the worker holding majors off (`GCQuiet.during`) 10 of 16 changes had a buffer of 8.8-11.8 ms (minor collections of the young arrays), without it 3 of 28 changes over 5 ms (6.8, 18.2, and 34.9 ms; no major counted in the two instrumented ones) and the rest 1.9-5.6 ms, so the workers leave GC alone.
- Cheaper stretches for the interim (Mac container, one channel, `interim_costs.rb` in the scratch notes; full = the shape-locked stretch): slices with ACID's rules 10-94 ms (plain slices 5-43 ms) against 0.37-1.55 s for the full stretch; the coarse vocoder 0.13-0.42 s (3-4x quicker; a 2048-sample window is no quicker).  Live, with both lanes sharing the GVL, the slice interim was ready 0.15-0.4 s after the change and the coarse one 0.3-0.9 s.  Not built, as findings: a per-buffer time-domain stand-in (WSOLA or ACID's per-slice position map read live from the current stretch or the file) would need no worker and follow ramps continuously; the live vocoder stream (`live: true`) already does that in the frequency domain at 6.5-8.4% of a core.
- Bench set `stretch-live-1-2026-11-22` (the private Bench set stretch-live-1, not published): three loops through a tempo jump, before (the measured stall spliced in as silence) and after with each interim; each interim held 6 s on three loops; a ramp.
