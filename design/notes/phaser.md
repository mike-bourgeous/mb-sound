# Phaser

<!-- summary -->
`sig.phaser(:vintage | :swirl | :ripple | :deep12 | :jet | :barberpole | rate, depth:, stages:, feedback:, mix:, center:, range:, spread:, shape:, sweep:, above:, amount:, direction:)` (`GraphNode::Phaser`, kernel `FastFilter.phaser` with an exact Ruby mirror) is a chain of 2-32 first-order allpass stages at one LFO-swept frequency, feedback around the chain solved in closed form, a linear dry/wet mix, Tone LFOs (sine or triangle, Hz or tempo-synced lengths), and stereo pairs whose right LFO runs `spread:` cycles ahead; `above:` keeps the lows dry (a Linkwitz-Riley split), `amount:` turns depth, mix, feedback, and stages from light to extreme, and `direction: :up/:down` makes a barber pole.
<!-- /summary -->

## Design (phaser-1, 2026-11-09)

The maintainer's request (2026-11-08): no phaser in the library, though the pieces existed (`filter(:allpass, cutoff: node)` with an LFO; fit_patch once built one ad hoc); a dedicated GraphNode with Params, `phaser(rate, depth:, stages:, feedback:, mix:, spread:, center:, range:)`, tempo-synced rates, even stages 2-12, signed feedback with a resonance limit, a mix, Blue Cat-style stereo spread, sweep center and range, and knobs onto which the plug-ins in his old ACID projects map (BluePhaser on a hi-hat, Blue Cat Phaser stereo and mono, VdPhazor, BassPhaserV10, an eFX Phaser).

- **Stages.**  Each stage is the topology-preserving (trapezoidal) one-pole allpass, H(s) = (wc - s) / (wc + s): v = (u - s) G, lp = v + s, s' = lp + v, y = 2 lp - u, with g = tan(pi fc / rate) from `mb_tan_pade` (only + - * /, so C and Ruby agree exactly) and G = g / (1 + g).  All stages share one frequency, as in the classic pedals (MXR Phase 90, Small Stone), so notches of N stages at fc sit where the chain's phase is an odd multiple of -180 degrees: tan(pi f / rate) = tan(pi fc / rate) tan((2k + 1) pi / (2N)), k = 0 ... N/2 - 1 (four stages: 0.414 fc and 2.414 fc; exact for the prewarped bilinear stages, spec'd within a 2.9 Hz bin from the rendered impulse response, which matches `Phaser.response` within 0.01 dB wherever the response is above -40 dB).  `filter(:allpass)` is the library's second-order SVF allpass; first-order stages give the classic unequal notch spacing and half the notches per stage pair, so the kernel has its own.  Odd stage counts raise (a lone stage leaves a tilt rather than a notch pair).
- **Feedback** goes from the chain's output to its input with no unit delay: each stage is linear in its input (y = (2G - 1) u + 2 (1 - G) s), so the chain is Y = A u + B and u = x + k Y solves to u = (x + k B) / (1 - k A) (zero-delay feedback, Zavalishin; the four-pole's method).  The closed loop is dry + wet H / (1 - k H): positive k peaks where H = 1 (the chain's phase a whole number of turns), negative k where H = -1, at 1 / (1 - |k|) over the wet level (spec'd; the rendered response matches the analysis within 0.02 dB at k = 0.7, -0.7, 0.95).  |k| is limited to 0.95 (+26 dB peaks): numbers past it raise, nodes are clamped in the kernel (NaN reads as 0).  A unit-delay loop would move the peaks off the analysis as the frequency rises; the closed form costs N multiply-adds per sample.  Stability: with |H| = 1 the static loop is stable for |k| < 1; a 30 Hz, 10-octave sweep of noise at k = 0.95 through 12 stages stays bounded (peak under 30 over 3 s, not growing; spec'd).  No level compensation for feedback (the pedals have none): on the Bench sources :jet measured +3.4 LU over the dry 909 beat (-10.3 vs -13.7 LUFS; :vintage -15.1, its notches take energy out), -0.1 LU on the saw pad (:vintage -2.6), and -0.7 LU on the hats.
- **Sweep**: the stages' frequency is center * 2 ** (range / 2 * depth * sweep), computed in the kernel per sample (exp; coefficients only when it changes) and clamped to 1 Hz .. 0.49 rate.  `center:` is Hz (a Pitch or node too), `range:` octaves at depth 1 (a node too) or a Range of frequencies for the sweep's ends (`200..5000`: center their geometric mean, range log2(hi / lo); with `center:` it raises), `depth:` 0..1 the share swept (the knob plug-ins call depth or sweep).  Octaves rather than Hz because phasers sweep geometrically and the plug-ins' min/max frequency pairs map onto a Range directly.
- **LFO**: a Tone LFO (`.lfo.sine` / `.lfo.triangle`, `with_phase(phase)`; built by `ModulationLfo.tone` since effects-2, shared with `flanger` and `rotary`) feeding the node's sweep input, so tempo sync is the existing machinery: `1.bar`, `2.bars`, `1.n4`, `knob(:len).bars` follow the tempo and lock to the timeline (`Sequence::Duration#lfo`); numbers and Pitches are Hz, a Length (`4.seconds`) is a period, a node is Hz.  `sweep:` replaces the LFO with any node of -1..1 (an envelope follower, a knob, a sample-and-hold) on both sides (with `spread:` it raises).
- **Stereo**: a mono input without spread gives one Phaser node; with `spread:` (cycles, 0.5 opposite) or a stereo input, two nodes, the right LFO started `spread` cycles ahead (exactly a left phaser with `phase: spread`, spec'd), returned as a Channels bundle (`ChannelDispatch::EXCLUDED`: the method handles bundles itself).
- **Mix**: linear, dry gain (1 - mix) dry, wet gain mix wet (`BlendMethods.gains(mix, :linear)`), default 0.5: the dry and wet are the same signal phase-shifted, so equal parts give full notches with peaks at 0 dB.  The delays' equal-power law (dry sqrt(1 - mix)) would put the same notches under +3 dB peaks; linear is the usual phaser law (question 3 in the phaser-1 report).  `dry:`/`wet:` are levels (Decibels, numbers, nodes) the mix multiplies; a negative wet inverts the wet polarity (Blue Cat's wet phase), turning notches into peaks.
- **Kernel and plans**: `FastFilter.phaser(out, x, sweep, center, range, depth, feedback, dry, wet, state, rate)` in fast_filter (no new source or header, so the memcheck map's structure is unchanged), exact Ruby mirror `Phaser.process_ruby` (`Kernels.register :phaser`).  The state is one double per stage plus the flush grid count (`MB_FLUSH_GRID`, 800 samples of the node's own time), so output is bit-identical at any buffer size (spec'd at 1/7/128/33 vs 800).  A plan boundary: one C call per buffer whose work dwarfs the call; fusing would need a fast_plan op and a lane-major op for a node that rarely sits inside a voice.  `node_traits memory: :short, carry: :adopt` (a rebuild with the same stage count keeps the stages' state).

Measured (Mac container, i9-9880H, 800-sample buffers, a moving sweep, ns per sample per channel, % of realtime at 48 kHz): kernel 43 / 71 / 98 ns at 4 / 8 / 12 stages (0.20 / 0.34 / 0.47%); the whole node with its Tone LFO about 48 / 77 / 107 ns (0.23 / 0.37 / 0.51%); the Ruby mirror 907 / 1231 / 1976 ns with YJIT (4.4 / 5.9 / 9.5%), 2042 / 2853 / 3686 without, so the C kernel was worth it.

## effects-3 (2026-11-14): crossover, amount, barber pole, 32 stages

From the maintainer's phaser-1 Bench notes (2026-11-13; brief `briefs/effects-3.md` in the workbench).

- **`above:`** ("Some of the variants cut out the kick too much, so a crossover option to only process above X hz might be good"): `phaser(..., above: f)` (Hz or a Pitch) splits the input with `#bands(f)` (Linkwitz-Riley 4th order) and phases only the high band, the low band summed back dry (`Phaser.crossover`; a Mixer per side reading both to the end, `stop_early: false`; a mono input with spread comes back stereo, the low band in both sides).  The low band passes through the split's allpass, not untouched; a notch tuned to 60 Hz takes a 60 Hz sine to under 0.05 without `above:` and leaves it within 0.03 of 1 with `above: 400` (spec).  A finite input (the arp file) ends cleanly, the same at 1/7/128/33/367-sample buffers as at 800 (the Crossover's lockstep trap noted in modulation.md didn't show: both bands are read every buffer by one Mixer).  Name: `above:` reads "phase above 150 Hz" (`mono_below` is the precedent); alternatives `dry_below:`, `low:`, `crossover:` (the rotary's split, where both bands are processed), `keep_lows:` (question).  The flanger could take the same option (question).
- **`amount:`** ("I'd want knobs to tweak to go from light to extreme across all the variants"): one Param, 0..1, 0.5 the preset unchanged (exactly: the formulas give each setting back at 0.5, and an absent amount builds nothing).  Mapping (`Phaser.amount_settings`; t = min(2a, 1), u = max(2a - 1, 0)): depth `v (1 - 0.75 (1 - t)) + (1 - v) u` (a quarter at 0, full at 1), mix `v (1 - 0.7 (1 - t)) + (max(v, 0.5) - v) u` (0.3 of itself at 0, the deepest notches at 1), feedback `v t + (+-0.9 - v) u` (none at 0, 0.9 with the preset's sign at 1, `AMOUNT_FEEDBACK`), and for a numeric amount stages `v 2^(u - (1 - t))` rounded to even within 2..32 (halved at 0, doubled at 1: :swirl 6 -> 4 / 12, :deep12 -> 24).  A node amount (a knob) moves depth, mix, and feedback live through the same formulas as node arithmetic; stages, structural, stay.  Settings given as nodes are left alone, and explicit numeric settings are scaled like the preset's.  Range and rate stay (question: should extreme also widen the range?).
- **`direction: :up / :down`** ("Could the :jet be modified to act like a risset tone / barber pole tone where it sounds like it's always rising or always falling?"): `Phaser.barber` builds `BARBER_CHAINS` (3) phasers per side, each swept once across the whole range per LFO cycle by an exact ramp (an aliased saw: LFO rates), a third of a cycle apart, each chain's wet faded by a raised cosine of the same LFO that is 0 where its ramp jumps back (aligned: the ramp jumps at phase 0.5, the window there is 0.0 to six places), the windows summing to 1 (2/3 each, within 2.4e-7), the dry signal once.  Held at the center (depth 0) the pole equals one phaser within 1e-4 (spec); :down mirrors :up in time (a held 1 kHz tone's level curve runs backward, spec).  Conservative choice: a new option usable with any preset plus a `:barberpole` preset (8 stages, 0.1 Hz, 6 octaves around 1 kHz, feedback 0.6, sine LFO for the windows, `direction: :up`), rather than changing `:jet` (`phaser(:jet, direction: :up)` gives the jet as a pole).  Measured direction (white noise through `:barberpole`, log-frequency spectra 0.1 s apart cross-correlated over 150-6000 Hz, 20 s): `:up` drifts +0.60 octaves per second (6 octaves per 10 s pass) with 190 upward frame shifts and none downward; `:down` -0.58 oct/s, 187 down and none up; `:jet` at 0.1 Hz -0.01 oct/s (97 up, 100 down: back and forth).  Three chains cost three phasers.  A sweep: node and a direction raise together.
- **32 stages** (the plug-in in his .acd song reads 32; he confirmed the 4-stage match by ear, "just a bit more feedback on the original phaser"): `STAGES` 2..32 even (the C kernel already took 64; no ext change).  The kernel matches the Ruby mirror at 32 stages, the rendered response the analysis (32 stages at 2 kHz: 16 notches, each within a bin), and noise swept over 10 octaves at 30 Hz with feedback 0.95 stays bounded (peak under 30 and not growing, as at 12).
- **Cost** (Mac container, YJIT, thread CPU per sample after a `110.hz.saw` source, best of 3 x 3 s, % of realtime; 128 / 800-sample buffers): 12 stages 150 / 110 ns (0.72 / 0.53%), 32 stages 266 / 244 ns (1.28 / 1.17%); `:jet` 124 / 106, `:jet, above: 150` 183 / 112 (the split's four SVFs and a Mixer); `:barberpole` 513 / 348 (2.46 / 1.67%: three chains and their window LFOs); `:swirl, amount: knob` 224 / 181 (stereo, the macro's node arithmetic).
