# Loop files, slice stretch, tempo-following loops

<!-- summary -->
`Sample['loop.wav']` reads loop metadata without ffmpeg (`Sample::Riff`: ACID tempo/beats/meter/root/one-shot, stretch markers or cue labels as `sample.markers`, `smpl` loops), then FLAC tags, then tempo and key words in names (`Sample::LoopInfo`, `sample.why(:bpm)`, `provenance(:bpm)`), or detects tempo and key (`detect: true`; `#detect_key`, `Analysis::Key`).  `stretch(r, mode: :slice)` (`Sample::SliceStretch`) moves slices to scaled times with exact attacks (`slices:`, `confirm:`, `slot_fade:`, `positions:`); `sample.follow` (`GraphNode::LoopPlayer`, a TimelineNode and plan boundary) plays a loop at the session tempo (slices for percussive loops, spectral for tonal ones, declared one-shots as recorded).  Console `loops(dir, bpm:, key:, beats:, detect:)` lists a folder from headers and names (keys match enharmonically).  Help `help :loops`.
<!-- /summary -->

## Quick reference

- `Sample['loop.wav']` reads ACID/`smpl`/cue metadata, tags, and names (`sample.why(:bpm)`, `sample.provenance(:bpm)`, `sample.summary`); `detect: true` or `#detect_key` measures; `stretch(r, mode: :slice, slices:, confirm:, fill:, fade:, slot_fade:, fade_law:, positions:)`; `sample.follow` plays in time with the session tempo.
- Console `loops(dir, bpm:, key:, beats:, detect:)`; help `help :loops`.
- A file's own loop info (below): `sample.markers` are its stretch markers (slice points) when it has them, else its cue points; a file that declares itself a one-shot (`sample.declared_one_shot?`, a setting of the Sample like `bpm`, fed by `LoopInfo`'s `declared_one_shot`) `follow`s as recorded (`stretch: :none`); `stretch(r, mode: :spectral, transients: :markers)` or `transients: [positions]`, with `confirm: true` for only those that have an attack; spec `spec/lib/mb/sound/sample_acid_spec.rb`.
- Data and description: what plays is read from the Sample's settings (`bpm`, `beats`, `loop_points`, `markers`, `declared_one_shot?`); `provenance(field)` / `why(field)` (and `meta_source`, their `via` words) say where each setting came from (`provenance(:markers).via == :strc`, `provenance(:declared_one_shot)`), `meta` holds the file's description and the facts nothing used (`meta[:source][:fields]`), and nothing plays or builds from either (`metadata.md`, the layers).  Format words (ACID, `strc`, `smpl`) stay in `Sample::Riff`, `LoopInfo`, and those provenance values.

The summary block above feeds the generated index (`design/notes/README.md`, `rake docs:index`).  Update it when behavior changes.

Branch `loops-1` (2026-10-15).  The maintainer: "If we add a slice-based stretch, could we start loading ACID metadata?  I have a large loop library."  Builds on the sampler (`sampler.md`: `Sample`, onsets, the cyclic stretch) and the spectral stretches (`granular.md`).

## API

```ruby
brk = Sample['~/Loops/Funk/break_98bpm.wav']   # ACID chunks, smpl, cues, tags, then names
brk.bpm  brk.beats  brk.bars  brk.meter  brk.key  brk.root  brk.markers  brk.meta_source
Sample['take3.wav', detect: true]              # tempo detection when nothing says (cached)
brk.detect_tempo                               # the same on any Sample
brk.stretch(1.25, mode: :slice)                # slices at the hits, attacks exact
brk.stretch(1.25, mode: :slice, fill: :stretch)   # each slice drawn out to the next hit instead of a gap (:even or :decay, picked per slice)
brk.stretch(0.8, mode: :slice, slices: :grid)  # markers / onsets / transients / grid / a Duration / a count / positions / Slices
brk.stretch(0.8, mode: :slice, slot_fade: 2/3r, fade_law: :power)   # faster: each cut slice crossfades over 2/3 of its slot into the next hit's lead-in
brk.stretch(0.8, mode: :slice, slices: 4, positions: [0, 0.97, 1.92, 2.89])   # a place per slice (seconds here: the second and fourth 10 ms late, a groove)
bg :brk, brk.follow                            # follows the session tempo (pitch kept, slice stretch)
bg :pad, pad.follow(stretch: :spectral)        # any stretch mode or a Hash of stretch options
bg :vinyl, brk.follow(:resample)               # pitch follows the tempo (varispeed)
brk.markers                                    # the file's stretch markers (its slice points), else cue points
bg :pad, pad.follow(stretch: { mode: :spectral, transients: :markers })   # the vocoder, attacks at the markers
bg :hit, shot.with(bpm: 120).follow            # shot.declared_one_shot?: as recorded from each loop start (stretch: :none)
brk.node(follow: true)                         # the same as follow
loops '~/Loops/Funk'                           # a listing (no audio decoded)
loops('~/Loops', bpm: 90..100, key: [:Am, :C], beats: 8)[0].sample.follow
```

Signatures: `Sample['Funk/break_98bpm.wav']` (`sample.markers`, `bpm`, `beats`, `bars`, `meter`, `key`, `meta_source`); `Sample[path, detect: true]` / `#detect_tempo` (Structure on the loop tiled to 8 s, cached); `stretch(r, mode: :slice, fill: :gap | :stretch, slices:)` (ratio 1 is exact; 4 ms fades and pre-rolls at every discontinuity; also `fade:`, `slot_fade:` and `fade_law: :linear | :power`, a crossfade by each cut slot's length, and `positions:`, a place per slice); `sample.follow(:stretch | :resample, stretch: :auto, align: :timeline | :launch)` (restretched per 1/4 BPM, cached); `loops(dir, bpm:, key:, beats:, detect:)` (never writes); `#key_analysis`, `#detect_key` (below).  Every stretch divides `bpm` by its ratio.  Help `help :loops`.

## Keys from the audio (listen-1, 2026-10-30)

`Sample#key_analysis(profile: :krumhansl | :temperley)` is an `Analysis::Key::Result` (cached per audio and region): the Krumhansl-Schmuckler method, the region's chromagram (`Analysis::Chroma`: 16384-point Hann frames every 4096 samples, 65 Hz to 5 kHz, folded onto 120 bins an octave, the tuning their circular mean, then 12 pitch classes at that tuning) summed over frames and correlated (Pearson) with the Krumhansl-Kessler major and minor profiles rotated to each tonic; the best of 24 is the key, with its correlation, the runner-up, the margin, and every score.  `Sample#detect_key(force: false)` sets `key` (`meta_source[:key] = :detected`) when the pick is clear (`Result#clear?`: r at least `MIN_CORRELATION` 0.7 and a margin of `MIN_MARGIN` 0.02) and `#material` isn't percussive; the reason is in `sample.why(:key)` either way.  `Sample[path, detect: true]` detects a missing tempo and key (`detect: :bpm`, `:key`, or an Array; other names raise with did_you_mean); `loops(dir, detect:)` does the same for listed files that nothing gave a tempo (loops only) or key, remembered per path, size, and mtime for the process, and `key:` filters match any spelling (`Analysis::Key.parse`/`.same?`: `:"A#m"` finds `:Bbm`; before, only the exact Symbol matched).  Keys print as `Sample#key` always has (`:Am`, `:"F#"`, `:Eb`): flats for F, Bb, Eb, Ab, Db major and Dm, Gm, Cm, Fm, Bbm, Ebm, sharps otherwise.  `Result#musical_key` gives a `MusicalKey` for the session key.

Measured (2026-10-30, `spec/lib/mb/sound/analysis_spec.rb` and a lab script): I-IV-V-I and harmonic-minor i-iv-V-i triad progressions (8-partial decaying tones, the root an octave down) in all 24 keys come out right with r 0.83-0.96 and margins 0.10-0.22, one of them 30 cents sharp (tuning found within 3 cents); a saw Am-F-G-Am clip 0.90 (next C major 0.77), the same over an 808 groove 0.89; drums alone: the jungle break 0.41, an 808 groove 0.66 (its tuned kick and snare read as D minor, margin 0.26: why the material check is there), a 909 groove 0.69 (margin 0.01), white noise 0.28.  The usual confusions of the method are the relative major/minor and the keys a fifth away (`scores` shows them).  Speed: about 100x realtime (a 180 s song in 1.85 s on the Mac container; the fold reads only the fifth of each spectrum inside 65 Hz to 5 kHz).

Not yet: key changes inside a sample (a windowed fold, `Chroma#fold(from:, to:)` is there for it), modes beyond major/minor, profiles for electronic music (Faraldo's EDM profiles), the attack-only listing shortcut for keys.

## RIFF chunks (`Sample::Riff`)

ffmpeg ignores the chunks loop libraries rely on, so a Ruby reader reads the headers and skips the data chunk.  Layouts (little-endian):

- `acid` (24 bytes; libsndfile's `wav_read_acid_chunk`): uint32 flags (0x01 one-shot, 0x02 root note set, 0x04 stretch, 0x08 disk-based, 0x10 high octave), uint16 root note, uint16 + float32 unknown, uint32 beats, uint16 meter denominator, uint16 numerator, float32 tempo.
- `smpl` (36 + 24 per loop): unity note, pitch fraction (of a semitone, /2^32), loops (type 0 forward, 1 ping-pong, 2 backward; end inclusive).
- `cue ` (4 + 24 per point; the sample offset is the position), `LIST`/`adtl` `labl`/`ltxt`, `LIST`/`INFO`.
- `strc` (ACID's stretch markers; no public description of its layout could be found, so it was worked out from loop files, "ACID loop info" below): a 28-byte header (uint32 28, uint32 entry count, five uint32 settings: the third the stretching method, 1 looping and 2 non-looping segments, the fifth the stretch spacing as a note division, 3 eighths and 4 sixteenths), then 32-byte entries: uint32 flags (0x01 disabled, 0x02 automatic), uint32 unused, uint64 sample position (frames at the file's rate), uint64 pin, uint32 value, uint32 unused.

Robustness: odd chunk sizes with or without the pad byte (an unpadded chunk is recognized by a chunk id starting right after it and none a byte later), RF64/BW64 (`ds64` data size), WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE (subformat from the GUID), unknown chunks, truncated files (data size clamped to the file), chunks over `MAX_CHUNK` (4 MB) skipped, counts clamped to their chunk, at most 10000 chunks.  `Riff.write` makes fixtures (specs write theirs at run time; no third-party loops in the repo).

## Metadata (`Sample::LoopInfo`, `Sample` attributes)

Order: our own tags (`Sample#save`) > ACID/smpl/cue chunks (WAV, and FLAC APPLICATION `riff` blocks from `flac --keep-foreign-metadata`) > tags (FLAC Vorbis comments BPM/TBPM/TEMPO, KEY/INITIALKEY; ffmpeg metadata) > names (file, then up to 3 folders) > detection, only when asked.  Explicit options win over all.

- Names: "98bpm", "98 BPM", "bpm140", a bare 60-200 number only when no "bpm" word appears and only one such number; keys as capitalized words (Am, F#m, Ebmaj7, C#min, "F minor"), a lone letter only next to a tempo ("Loop A 90").
- `beats`: the file's count (ACID) while the region is the whole file, else tempo × length (whole within 2%, else a Float).  `bars` = beats / meter numerator.  ACID one-shots keep no tempo.
- ACID root (when the root-set flag is on) sets `root`; `smpl` sets the root (unity note minus pitch fraction) and the loop; an ACID loop without a `smpl` loop loops the whole file (`:forward`).  Positions scale from the file's rate to 48 kHz.
- `markers`: `Sample::Marker(position, label)`, play-order positions from the region start; views keep them, edits scale them.  A file's stretch markers (labels nil; `provenance(:markers).via == :strc`) when it has them, else its cue points (`:cue`); the cue points of a file whose stretch markers are the markers stay description (`meta[:source][:fields]['cue.point[0]']`, at the file's own rate).
- Every stretch now divides `bpm` by the ratio (before loops-1 a stretched bounce kept its old tempo for Durations).
- `detect_tempo`: `Structure.analyze` on the loop tiled to at least 8 s (the loop repeats seamlessly, so the tracker sees several cycles), rounded so the loop holds whole beats when within 5%; the analysis is cached per audio and region.

## Loop or one-shot, and name tempos that fit (round 2, maintainer 2026-10-15)

Maintainer: bare numbers in names "should check that the length of the file plausibly matches the tempo, and default to one-shot below a certain length"; "I don't think ACID loops ever loop just a subset."

- **Name tempos must fit** (`LoopInfo.fit_tempo`): every tempo in the file name and up to 3 folders (numbers next to "bpm", else every bare 60-200 number), and each one's double and half (double/half-time names), counts only if the length is a whole number k ≥ 1 of its beats within max(5 ms, 1.5% of a beat) (a trimmed tail or padded end of a few ms still fits).  Best score wins: the fit error over the tolerance, +0.75 for double/half time, +0.5 unless k is 1/2/4/8/16/32/64 or whole bars, +0.25 for bare numbers, +0.1 per folder level up.  The reason is kept in words (`entry.why[:bpm]`, `sample.why(:bpm)`): "name 98 (fits 8 beats)", "name 87 as 174 (double time, fits 3 beats)", "name 98 (no fit to 3.300 s)" (no tempo then; `detect: true` still works).  Without a known length (listing an mp3) the first explicit tempo, or the only bare number, counts unchecked ("length unknown"); `Sample.from_file` passes the decoded length.  ACID and tag tempos are not checked.
- **Loop or one-shot** (`LoopInfo.classify`, `entry.one_shot` true/false/nil, `sources[:one_shot]`, `why[:one_shot]`), first rule that applies:
  1. ACID's one-shot flag, always.
  2. A `smpl` loop (non-ACID files): a loop at those points.
  3. A word in the nearest name that has one (file name, then folders): loop words (loop, groove, break, beat, pattern, phrase, riff, top, fill, plurals) win over one-shot words (kick, snare, clap, rim, tom, crash, cymbal, cowbell, hit, shot, "one shot", stab, fx, sfx, impact, zap) in the same name, so "Kick Loop" is a loop and "One Shots/Loop 3.wav" a loop; a folder's loop word doesn't make a file shorter than `SHORT` a loop ("Loops/blip.wav").
  4. Shorter than `TINY` (0.25 s, one beat at 240 BPM): a one-shot.
  5. A name tempo that fits: a loop at that tempo.
  6. Shorter than `SHORT` (0.75 s, one beat at 80 BPM, the slowest common loop tempo: a sound shorter than that with no fitting tempo is a hit): a one-shot.
  7. In `Sample.from_file` only (it has the audio), files rules 1-4 didn't decide and rule 5 called loops: one hit decaying to silence is a one-shot (at most one onset, within 10 ms of the start, and the last tenth 30 dB below the loudest 10 ms; `Sample.decays?`).
  One-shots keep no name tempo (the reason says "dropped: a one-shot").  Loops play `:forward` over the whole file unless a `smpl` loop applies; **ACID loops always loop the whole file**, keeping any `smpl` loops only as description (`sample.meta[:source][:fields]['smpl.loop[0]']`); smpl loops still apply to non-ACID files (sampler instruments).
- `loops()` shows `kind` (loop, one-shot, -) and `why` (`Entry#reason`).  `LoopInfo.scan` ignores folder names above the listed folder (a library root called "Loops" says nothing about one file).

Ideas not built (cheap signals for later):
- An "attack-only" check in listings (read the first and last 100 ms of PCM WAVs directly; no ffmpeg) so `loops()` can apply rule 7.
- A loop seam check: a loop's end flows into its start (small step at the seam, similar spectra) where a one-shot's tail doesn't.
- Onset spacing: onsets on a regular grid at a fitting tempo (strong evidence of a loop, and a tempo for nameless files without full tempo detection).
- Folder conventions of big libraries ("Drum Hits", "Loops/Drums", "Construction Kits"), and sample-pack name prefixes ("XX_120_Am_Bass_Loop_01").
- Key words for one-shots ("bass C2" as root rather than key) and Apple Loops / AIFF `basc` flags.

## Slice stretch (`Sample::SliceStretch`, `stretch(r, mode: :slice)`)

ACID/REX-style: cut at the hits, start slice i at round(r × its start), copy it exactly.

- Slices: markers (a file's stretch markers, else cue points), else onsets (Sample::Onsets), else sixteenths at the sample's bpm; or `slices:` given.
- `slices: :transients` (stretch-fixes-3, 2026-11-14; `Sample#transient_cuts`): cuts only where a transient is, so every hit stays whole and lands on its stretched time.  With markers past the start (a loop file's stretch markers), the detected onsets that a marker (or the start) confirms within `confirm:` (true: `Sample::TRANSIENT_REACH`, 15 ms; or a length), each on its attack; without markers, or `confirm: false`, every detected onset; `threshold:` is the detector's (9 dB).  It replaced round 2's `slices: :beats` (one slice a beat, at the beat's marker or the grid; removed), after the maintainer heard it on a tonal drum loop with a shaker at 2x: "By 'beats' I meant transients.  As is this just breaks the timing of the loop."  Measured (the study's source renders with their project markers; a cut "through loud sound" is within 20 dB of the loop's loudest 10 ms with no onset within 15 ms; hit timing: where each detected onset's attack lands against ratio × its time, with `fill: :gap`):

  | Loop | Cuts | Through loud sound | 1-8 ms into an attack | Hits off their time: median, worst |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tonal drum loop 2x, every marker | 49 | 5 | 12 | 0.3, 7.1 ms |
  | Tonal drum loop 2x, beats (removed) | 12 | 1 | 0 | 341, 682 ms |
  | Tonal drum loop 2x, transients | 39 | 0 | 0 | 0, 0 |
  | Drum loop 1.18x, every marker | 17 | 7 | 3 | 0.8, 1.4 ms |
  | Drum loop 1.18x, beats (removed) | 5 | 0 | 3 | 1.4, 20 ms |
  | Drum loop 1.18x, transients | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0, 0 |

  The length is round(ratio × length) in every case.  On these two loops every detected onset has a marker within 15 ms, so `:transients` equals `:onsets` there; `:onsets` also takes softer rises near the sixteenth grid when the sample has a bpm (#slice_onsets), `:transients` only the detector's.  The markers sit within 1 ms of their onsets mostly, but 12 of the tonal drum loop's sit 1-7 ms after them, so cutting at every marker leaves those attacks' first milliseconds on the slice before.
- Round 4 (2026-11-14), his verdict on the drum loop at 1.18x: "B or F for this one.  Original A pulls the post-snare kick early."  Measured, each hit's landing against ratio × its time (the source's 12 ms from the hit searched in the output; hits: the nine onsets at 4 dB, five of them at 9 dB):
  - A at every stored marker: within 5.4 ms, but two hits are cut in two.  The marker for the kick before beat 3 sits 361 samples (7.5 ms) after its attack, so the attack is the last 7.5 ms of the slice before, which starts 4172 samples earlier: it plays 751 samples (15.6 ms) before the kick's body.  The snare on beat 4 the same (its marker 380 samples late: 14.9 ms).  That is the kick he heard pulled early.
  - `:transients` as round 3 built it (the 9 dB onsets a marker confirms: 5 cuts) leaves the four softer hits inside slices, 14, 20, 29, and 32 ms early: a hit that gets no cut keeps its distance d from the cut before it and lands (ratio - 1) × d early.
  - Now `:transients` cuts at every onset the detector is sure of and at the softer ones (4 dB, `SOFT_ONSET`) a marker stands for within `confirm:`, each on its attack: 7 cuts on this loop, both split hits whole and on time, seven of the nine hits exact; the two soft hits with no marker within 15 ms are 20 ms early.  `confirm: false, threshold: 4.db` cuts at all nine (all exact) at the price of trusting every 4 dB rise.
  - What happens to a hit no cut marks, stated: it keeps its distance from the cut before it (early when slowing, late when speeding up, by |ratio - 1| × that distance).  The fills do not put it right at small ratios: the even fill carries on unstretched for its first cycles (the two soft hits still 20 ms early at 1.18x under `fill: :stretch`), and a marker slice 90 ms long bounds the error at 16 ms.  So timing wants cuts, and where the markers come from a loop file (the branch with the stretch markers) the softer threshold near markers is what finds them.
  - Still open (a question for him): `slices: nil` on a sample with markers cuts at the markers as stored, which splits hits whose markers are late; `:transients` could be that default once the stretch markers are merged.  Merged together (stretch-batch-1, 2026-11-15) both ways stay: `slices: :markers, confirm: true` (cuts only at the markers that have an attack, below) and `slices: :transients` (also cuts at the onsets the detector is sure of with no marker near), one `Sample::TRANSIENT_REACH` (15 ms) for "a marker stands for this onset"; which becomes the default is his choice.
- Slowing down: `fill: :gap` (default) plays the slice's own audio, fading out over `fade:` (4 ms) before its end, then silence until the next hit; `fill: :stretch` draws each slice out to the next hit with a cyclic stretch after 15 ms of attack, `:even` or `:decay` as `SliceStretch.fill_kind` picks per slice (below; both can be asked for by name; slices shorter than the attack plus two cycles keep a quarter of themselves and cycle half of the rest; under 5 ms a slice plays with a gap).
- Speeding up: each slice is cut at the next one's new time with the same fade (or by its slot: `slot_fade:`, below).
- Pre-rolls: a slice that doesn't follow on from the one before (as in the input) gets the input's last `fade` samples before its hit faded in ahead of it, crossfading with the outgoing fade, so the attack is exact and joins what led into it (no step out of silence).  The first slice's pre-roll comes from the loop's end, at the output's end, so seamless loops stay seamless.
- Ratio 1 gives the input back exactly.  Reversed samples stretch in play order.  Pure Ruby + Numo; a two-bar 909 loop takes 3-13 ms (`:gap`); `:stretch` took 130-150 ms on a 2 s loop of eight slices (Linux, 2026-11-13).

### Slot crossfades and positions (stretch-fixes-4, 2026-11-14; options, the default is unchanged, waiting for the maintainer's ears)

Measured on renders of sliced loops played faster by a loop-based sequencer (the study behind the importer branch; four drum loops at ratios 0.944 and 0.528, 1,269 of 1,361 hit instances within 0.25 ms of the rule): a slice cut short is not truncated.  With its slot S (the next cut's new place minus its own) it plays from its cut as in the file for the first third of S, then crossfades at equal power (a cosine out, a sine in) over the other two thirds into the audio that leads up to the next cut, arriving there in step.  So a hit on a cut is exact, and a hit that leads into a cut (a ghost note, a grace kick) keeps its distance to that cut.  The fixed fade does something else to such a hit: the slice plays on from its cut, so the hit comes out (1 - ratio) × its distance from that cut late, or not at all when its slot ends first, and only the last 4 ms before each cut are the input's (`fade:`; capped at half the slice going out but not coming in, so a slot shorter than the fade has a pre-roll longer than itself).

- `slot_fade:` (nil, or a share from 0 to 1) is that crossfade for slices cut short: the slice plays as it is for (1 - share) of its slot, then fades out over the rest while the input's last share × S samples before the next cut fade in over the same samples; fade-out and fade-in the same length, never longer than the slot.  The last slot fades into the loop's end, which leads into the first cut when the loop repeats.
- `fade_law:` is how every fade pair of the slice stretch adds up: `:linear` (default: raised cosines, gains summing to 1) or `:power` (their square roots, a quarter sine and cosine: squares summing to 1), the names of `blend(curve:)`.  It applies to the fixed fades too (a slowed loop's 4 ms fades change shape under `:power`).
- The measured rule is `slot_fade: 2/3r, fade_law: :power`; both are plain settings, so other shares and the linear law are there to hear.
- What does not change with them: ratio 1 (the input), slices drawn out (ratio above 1: gaps, fills, and their fixed fades; slowed loops were not measured), slices that follow on.  Without them the output is what it was, bit for bit (checked against the code before the change on 108 combinations of ratio, fill, and fade; the golden set).
- `positions:` (one per cut, in the cuts' order; `Sample#stretch`: Integers are output samples, numbers seconds, Lengths, Durations at the stretched tempo) puts each slice at its own place instead of round(ratio × cut), for slices moved off the stretch's grid (a groove).  Each slot is then cut short (`slot_fade:` or the fixed fade), drawn out (`fill:`), or left to follow on, by its own length against the slice's; the length stays round(ratio × length).  Limits: positions run forward with their cuts, and a cut at the start stays at 0 (moving the loop's first hit would need the slot before it, the loop's last, to wrap round).  Markers of the result still scale by the ratio.
- Specs (`sample_loop_spec.rb`): a per-slot Ruby reference of the rule within 1e-6 (-120 dB; single precision) for shares 2/3, 1/4, 1 and both laws at 0.8x and 0.528x, stereo; hits on cuts exact for the slot's first third; a click 10 ms before each cut and before the loop's end lands 10 ms before it within 4% of its level (cut off by the fixed fade); a 100-sample slice whose slot (80) is shorter than the 192-sample fade; ratio 1 and 1.25x untouched; a loop with one slot cut and one drawn out under `positions:`; `follow(stretch: { ..., slot_fade:, fade_law: })` through the loop point at 800- and 367-sample buffers (128 in the slow tier).  Sweep: two rows in the graph sweep's loop players (`spec/support/graph_sweep.rb`).
- Open (questions for him): the names; whether sped-up loops should take the measured rule by default (a Bench row on the importer branch); a groove's own rule for where slices go.

### Filled slices (stretch-fixes-1, 2026-11-13; round 2 2026-11-14)

The maintainer on the stretch study's Bench set (a generated drum loop at 1.25x, slices with filled decays): "sounds really bad, like there's a bug in how it's lining up the stretch".  Measured on that loop (16 slices of about 166 ms; its kick is a 20-40 Hz body about 100 ms long that stops abruptly, with a quiet rumble after it):

- Hits land where they should: every slice's first 15 ms is the input's, sample for sample, at round(cut × ratio), and the fill follows on from it with no step (the first cycle starts on the input's next sample).  No fill plays the next slice's audio.
- The fill was the fitted cyclic stretch of everything after 15 ms.  Its splice search prefers the place that simply carries on (correlation 1) while that is within reach, so the first 40-65 ms after each hit played unstretched, and then the plan caught up in jumps of 12-25 ms back into the kick's body: its last lobe played again 115 ms after the hit, 20 dB above the decay around it, at -9 to -11 dB re the loop's peak (5 of 16 hits at 1.25x; at 2x a step of up to +26 dB to -5 dB, 34 steps of 6 dB or more).  The last cycle was forced to the end of the slice (jumps of up to 25 ms), and the fill stopped up to 14 ms short of the slice's end.
- So: not a mis-set offset, but a second, off-grid hit made of the first one's body (a hit's body is not a decay that can be cycled).

`fill: :stretch` now (`SliceStretch.fill_slice`):
- A time map by level (`fill_map`): each sample's share of the stretch is 0 where the slice's envelope is within 6 dB of its loudest (`FILL_HOLD`), full where it has fallen 20 dB or more (`FILL_DECAY`), in proportion between, plus 2% everywhere (`FILL_EVEN`: a held note is drawn out evenly).  A hit's body, and any other hit the slice holds, keeps its length; the quiet after it takes the stretch.
- The envelope (`SliceStretch.envelope`) is the analytic signal's magnitude, squared and smoothed by two 10 ms moving averages (a short RMS ripples at twice a kick's frequency).
- A cyclic plan anchored at both ends (`Lofi.anchored_plan`: equal cycles, each starting where the input best matches what followed the one before, within the usual search radius of its place on the map; the last ends at the slice's end or up to two radii before it).
- Every cycle's level follows the envelope read at the mapped time (`Lofi.play_plan(gain:)`, -36 to +6 dB), so a cycle that repeats a louder part is turned down to where the decay has got to.

## ACID loop info drives `follow` and `stretch` (acid-loop-info-1, 2026-11-10; held for the maintainer's Bench verdict)

The maintainer's decision (2026-11-10): "ACID loop info drives `follow`.  The WAV `acid` chunk's one-shot flag means no stretch.  Parse the `strc` chunk [...] into markers, so `follow` / `stretch` on any ACIDized loop gets ACID's slice points, not only inside the converter."  It changes what ACIDized loops do by default, so it waits for a Bench A/B.

- **Stretch markers are the markers.**  `Sample::Riff::Strc` (`Riff.read(path).strc`: `count`, `settings`, `entries` of `StrcEntry(flags, position, pin, value)`, `lists`, `markers`, `stretch_method`, `spacing`, `forced(frames, beats)`) reads the chunk (the facts `strc.count`, `strc.settings`, `strc.entry[i]`: `Facts.of_riff`); `LoopInfo` consumes them and makes its markers the file's `markers` (`sources[:markers] == :strc`, `why[:markers]`: "strc: 17 stretch markers (5 forced divisions)"; a Sample's `provenance(:markers)` names `strc.entry[0]`, the chunk, and its offset), scaled to the Sample's rate like every position.  They outrank cue points: in the 46 library files with both, the cue points are bar regions with labels or the first and last frame, not slice points (they stay in `Entry#cues`, and as `cue.point[i]` facts nothing used in the Sample's `meta[:source][:fields]`).  So `stretch(r, mode: :slice)` and `follow`'s slice stretch cut at ACID's markers by default, as they already did at cue markers (`slices:` given still wins), and `LoopPlayer#stretch_reason` says so ("slice: name 'drum', at 31 markers": the count, from `Sample#markers`; which kind of marker a file gave is provenance, `provenance(:markers)` and `why(:markers)`, and `follow` doesn't read it).
- **One-shots play as recorded.**  `follow`'s `stretch: :auto` picks `:none` for a Sample whose file declares itself a one-shot: `Sample#declared_one_shot?` ("none: a one-shot by its file's loop info plays as recorded").  The flag is data, a setting of the Sample beside `bpm` and `beats` (2026-11-11, the maintainer: "guard against metadata becoming interpreted as data", and "ACID" as a concept stays at the edges): `LoopInfo` turns the `acid` chunk's one-shot flag into `Entry#declared_one_shot` (true, false for a declared loop, nil when the file doesn't say; the only declaration read today), `Sample.from_file` makes it the Sample's `declared_one_shot:` setting, views, `with`, and edits keep it, and `Sample[data, declared_one_shot: true]` or `with(declared_one_shot: false)` set it by hand.  Where it came from is `provenance(:declared_one_shot)` (via `:acid`, the fact `acid.flags`, why "the file's one-shot flag is set"; `:saved` for a Sample read back from its own tags), beside `provenance(:one_shot)` for LoopInfo's wider call (flag, name, length, decay), which sets how the Sample plays (`one_shot?`); before this the decision read the description (a Sample given other `meta:` changed how it played).  `stretch(r, mode: :none)` (aliases `:unstretched`, `:as_recorded`) is the building block, an edit like the other modes: the sample at its own speed in `r` times its length, zeros after it when longer, a 4 ms fade-out (`fade:`) at the cut when shorter; the LoopPlayer reads it like any stretch, so the hit starts again at every loop start.  Like every stretch it is read at floor(phase × length): sample for sample when the loop lasts a whole number of samples, else one sample is repeated or dropped per pass (a 15967.2-sample loop period played a real one-shot one sample late after its first sample).  On clocks its ratio is exact (other modes round to `CLOCK_STEP`, which would drop or repeat a sample every few thousand).  ACID one-shots keep no tempo, so `follow` still needs a length: `shot.with(bpm: 120)` or `shot.clocked(2.seconds)`.  `follow(stretch: :slice)`, `follow(:resample)`, and `stretch(...)` called directly do what they are asked.  One-shots by name, length, or decay (LoopInfo's other rules) are not covered by the decision and follow by their material as before.  `Sample#save` keeps the flag (the tag `mb_sample_declared_one_shot`), so a graph document that bundles the sample as a FLAC file plays it the same (a bundle round trip lost it before the tag: the loaded graph stretched the hit in slices).
- **`transients:` takes positions.**  `stretch(r, mode: :spectral | :paul, transients:)`: `true` (`:onsets`) detects as before; `:markers` takes the sample's markers (detected onsets when it has none past its start); an Array of positions as `slices:` takes them (samples, seconds, Lengths, Durations, Markers; play order) or a `Slices`; `false` (or `[]`) none.  `Spectral.vocoder`'s `onsets:` did the work already.  Reversed samples map positions to stored order.  `slices:` takes Markers in its lists too.
- **`confirm:` keeps the markers that have an attack, on the attack** (stretch-fixes-1, 2026-11-13; waits for the maintainer's ears).  The maintainer on the stretch study's Bench set (a generated drum loop at 1.25x): the vocoder guided by "the markers that have an attack" (the study's own filter: a marker with a detected onset within 15 ms, used at the marker's position, the loop start left out) "splits on some decays" that the vocoder with its own onsets does not.  Measured: every one of those 13 markers has an onset the detector fires on at its own threshold, so nothing in the list is a decay by that test; but four sit 5.2 to 9.3 ms before their hits (grid positions), where the level over ±10 ms falls or barely moves at three of them (-16.3, -8.7, -1.7 dB; +10.8 dB at the fourth; the hits themselves are soft ones in a loud kick's decay, steps of -4 to +13 dB at their onsets), and the list has no position for the loop's first hit.  The vocoder's unstretched span (64 ms before a position, 43 ms after) and its phase-reset frames sit that much early with them: moving each marker onto its onset changed the decay before three of those four hits by 13 to 18 dB for 40 to 100 ms, and the first kick, stretched like a pad without its start position, was 12 dB down for 90 ms of every pass.  `stretch(r, mode: :spectral, transients: :markers | positions, confirm: true | reach)` (`Sample#confirmed_onsets`, `Sample::TRANSIENT_REACH` 15 ms) therefore keeps the detected onsets within reach of a given position or of the region's start: the positions with an attack, each on its attack.  Against the vocoder with its own onsets (10 ms RMS envelopes every 5 ms, where either is above -45 dB): the generated loop at 1.25x, every marker 5.8 dB rms (over 6 dB for 600 ms of 2.8 s), the study's filter 4.5 dB (355 ms), `confirm: true` 2.6 dB (90 ms: the one onset no marker is near); a recorded guitar phrase at 0.87x 2.2, 1.3, 1.3 dB (four onsets without a marker); a sustained pad, a sax riff, and a drum loop: `confirm: true` is the vocoder's own result bit for bit (the pad's 15 grid markers leave only its start).  So confirmed markers are the detector's onsets, thinned to the ones a marker stands for: no better than the plain vocoder where its detector is right (the maintainer picked the plain vocoder on the drum loop and the guitar phrase), and the way to keep it from firing where no marker is (a saw bass's ripple, above).  No strength or rise-time rule was needed to tell attacks from decays on this material.  His verdict (Bench, 2026-11-14): on the drum loop he picked "after: every marker, confirm: true" over the study's filter, the plain vocoder, and every marker; on the guitar phrase "same".  Round 2: `confirm:` also filters the slice stretch's cut points (`stretch(r, mode: :slice, slices: :markers | positions, confirm: true | reach)`): his "A is wrong because there are markers in the middle of drum hit decays" (a drum loop: 6 of its 16 markers cut through sound within 20 dB of the loudest with no onset there; its confirmed cuts are its 5 detected attacks).
- **Tonal loops keep the plain spectral stretch** under `follow` (the listening study decides whether marker-guided spectral becomes a default); it is one option away: `follow(stretch: { mode: :spectral, transients: :markers })`.

The `strc` chunk, as read (`Sample::Riff::Strc`; 18058 WAV files of the maintainer's loop library, 13385 with the chunk, and the stretch markers his ACID projects hold for the same files):

- Verified (864 projects, 1641 takes with stretch markers for a library file that has the chunk): the header is 28 bytes and says so; entries are 32 bytes to the end of the chunk (one ACID generation leaves 8 zero bytes after them); the uint64 at +8 is the marker's sample position at the file's own rate (48 kHz files fit their own rate, not 44.1 kHz): 84% of the 83333 markers read are the source sample of a marker in the projects (projects move and drop markers by their own settings; 825 of the 1641 lists are the same marker for marker), and the audio's high-frequency energy rises by more than 6 dB at 36% of 13482 markers in 400 loops of 19 packs against 2% of the points halfway between markers (52% against 1% in the older one-list files; the rest are grid divisions in held sound); flag 0x01 markers are disabled (1 of 1318 is among the projects' markers); the header's stretching method and spacing match the projects' settings for the same files (1572 of 1609 and 514 of 521 takes).
- Likely: a pin (+16) that differs from its position is the marker's time on the grid, in frames (the projects pin 543 of 1734 such markers at that time; where a marker was moved, the audio rises at +8 and not at +16: a median 3 to 6 dB against 0.5 dB over 7479 moved markers).
- Against `Sample#onsets` on 28 loops of 15 packs (every chunk generation, 44.1 and 48 kHz): all marker lists rising and inside the file; 26% of 837 markers within 10 ms of a detected onset and 38% of 560 onsets within 10 ms of a marker (percussive loops agree hit for hit, 8 of 9 and 11 of 15 markers; tonal loops have markers on the sixteenth grid where the detector finds nothing, or ripple onsets between markers).  So markers and detected onsets are different slice points: what the Bench A/B is for.
- The chunk holds several lists one after the other, each sorted by position (a new list starts where the position goes down): in newer files the detected transients with float32 strengths ("hints": flags 2, pin 0), copies of an automatic marker list (flags 2, pin 0, value 0), then the stretch markers with pins.  Assumed: the markers are the last list that isn't hints; when that list holds only hand-set markers (no flag 0x02 entry), the automatic ones are in the list before it (the two together gave a whole grid wherever looked at).
- The header's count is taken as the end of what its writer described: the oldest files (settings all zero) often carry more entries after the counted ones (1400 files), appended by a later ACID version without changing the count; in a sample of 40 projects their markers agreed with the counted list more often (the same list in 4 of 8 and 3 of 4 files of two such shapes, against 1 and 0 for the appended lists).
- Older files (one list; 39% of the chunks) hold only detected markers, a median of 2 a beat, and ACID adds its "force divisions at" grid when playing; newer files have the divisions in the list (a median of 3.9 a beat).  `Strc#forced` adds the spacing grid for one-list files where no marker is within a quarter of a division (ACID keeps a division unless a marker is within about 2 ms, which would give slices of a few ms), for loops with ACID's beat count; 3378 library files get some.  Without them 724 of 5231 such files had under one marker a beat.
- Chunks made for other audio: 265 files have markers past their end (copies at half or a quarter of the rate, or rendered at another tempo, that kept the original's chunks); their markers are left out (`why[:markers]` says so).  A marker on the last frame is dropped.
- Not decoded: the header's other settings (65/5 and 25/10 pairs, a percentage), the 16.16 fraction one generation keeps per marker, the first marker's pin in the oldest files (the audio's first hit, a few ms in).  Not used yet: pins (ACID plays a moved marker's audio at its pinned time, so slices could be placed by pin instead of by `r` times their start), hint strengths, the stretching method.
