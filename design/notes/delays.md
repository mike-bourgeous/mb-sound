# Delays

Detailed notes moved from CLAUDE.md (2026-10-10); CLAUDE.md keeps a summary and points here.  Update both when behavior changes.


`Filter::Delay` (`#delay`) and `GraphNode::MultitapDelay` (`#multitap`) are front ends for one `MB::Sound::DelayLine` (`lib/mb/sound/delay_line.rb`): a circular buffer that grows without losing stored audio, read in C (`MB::Sound::FastDelay.read` / `.feedback`) with Ruby mirrors (`#read_ruby` / `#feedback_ruby`) that specs check for exactly equal values.  Interpolation (`interpolation:`) defaults to `:sinc` (`DelayLine::DEFAULT_INTERPOLATION`; Kaiser-windowed, cutoff lowered by the read speed when a moving delay raises the pitch, so no aliasing up to 4x); `:cubic` and `:linear` (the old lo-fi sound) are options.  Constant whole-sample delays read directly in every mode; a moving or fractional `:sinc` delay costs ~1.5-2% of realtime per mono delay.  No mode reads samples newer than the current input.  Delay times are any length (`0.01`, `250.ms`, `96.samples`, `3.n16`, nodes, `node.samples`) held as `Length::Source`, so they keep their unit when the sample rate changes and rate changes rebuild nothing; fractional constant delays are kept (snapped within 1e-9 of whole samples).  `feedback:`, `wet:`, and `dry:` may be numbers or graph nodes; `#delay` smooths delay changes by default (`smooth:`), `#multitap` only with `smooth:` (`smoothing:` is the older spelling, still accepted; 2026-10-13).  `delay(seconds: t)` is deprecated (warns once per call site; graph documents load it silently, and export prints `delay(t)`).  With a block (`sig.delay(t, feedback: g) { |fb| insert }`) the feedback runs through the block one sample at a time (see Feedback loops), compensated with `compensate: :echo` (low cuts and bandpasses count as wires, so echoes land on k * t).  Tools: `bin/delay_gallery.rb` (null-test cases), `bin/delay_benchmark.rb` (`--interpolation`), `bin/delay_quality.rb` (error and aliasing against an exact sine answer).

Ringing out (2026-10-13, branch fixes-2): `#delay` (with or without feedback), `#multitap`, and feedback loops (so the tape-echo form too) keep playing after their input ends, instead of ending with it (a one-shot pluck through `delay(1.n8.dotted, feedback: 0.5)` used to play nothing in a Session, whose tempo delay started at 0.375 s, after the pluck had ended).  The input's end (nil, or a short last buffer) feeds silence in; the node ends on the exact sample where what it has written into its delay lines (the feedback, or a loop's output) has stayed below -90 dB (`MB::Sound::RingOut::THRESHOLD`, the same as `Session::Master::TAIL_THRESHOLD`) for its longest delay plus `DelayLine::MARGIN` (the sinc reach), so nothing louder can come out later and the tail has the same length at any buffer size.  A plain delay or multitap therefore ends one delay (+50 samples) after its input; what is cut is below -90 dB times wet/feedback.  `Filter::Delay#ring_out(count)` is the hook (called by `Filter::SampleWrapper` once its source ends, only for filters that define it and have no extra inputs); `MultitapDelay#frame_input` pads with silence; `FeedbackLoop` reads ended boundary inputs as silence (an ended modulator input reads as 0 too) and watches its output.  The tape form's dry mix and pipeline sum use Mixers with `stop_early: false` (`DelayMethods.ring_sum`) so the input's end doesn't cut the echoes.  `delay(t, ring_out: false)` ends with its input instead (Reverb's predelay and show_internals delays use it, since its `extra_time` already makes the tail).  A loop gain of 1 or more never decays, so the tail plays on (Session tails and `write` cap it).  Effects after a `Ringdown` (effect scripts) and graphs whose input never ends (clips, live input) are unchanged.  Also fixed: tempo-synced delays sampled outside a Session started at 0 samples and glided up (the start reset ran before the smoother existed).

Delay networks, ping-pong, Haas, tape echoes (delay-fx-1, 2026-10-28; A1 and a light A2 of the 2026-10-03 delay feedback proposal).  `MB::Sound::FastDelay.network` (fast_delay.c; `DelayLine.network`, exact Ruby mirror `DelayLine.network_ruby`) runs L real delay lines and T taps one sample at a time: every line is written with its input, every tap reads its line (as `#feedback` reads its one tap: whole-sample delays directly unless sinc, the sinc read speed from the tap's previous delay), then each line adds `gain[line][tap] * tap` for every gain (numbers or per-sample buffers; in tap order, in double precision).  One line, one tap, and gains `[[g]]` are `#feedback` bit for bit (spec).  `GraphNode::DelayNetwork` (MultiOutput: one `Tap` per tap; inputs per line, nodes or nil; taps `[line, time]` with any delay time and optional smoothing; gains numbers or nodes) builds on it; the first tap read in a buffer runs the network; output and ending are the same at any buffer size; after every input ended it rings out with `RingOut` over what the lines get back (no feedback: one longest delay plus `DelayLine::MARGIN`, as MultitapDelay).  A plan boundary; `Synth.long_memory?` counts it.
- `multitap(t1, t2, ..., feedback: [g1, g2, ...] | g)`: regenerating taps on one line (a tape echo with several playback heads).  One gain is the loop's as a whole, shared evenly (each tap `g / taps`, so any gain below 1 decays; decision taken conservatively, open question); without `feedback:` the plain MultitapDelay (unchanged; zero gains give its samples and length exactly).
- `ping_pong(time = 1.n8, feedback: 0.5, spread: 1, dry: 1, wet: 1, mix: nil, input: :mono | :stereo, smooth: true, interpolation:)` (alias `pingpong`; `StereoDelayMethods`, GraphNode and Channels, not run per channel): two lines feeding each other (`[[0, g], [g, 0]]`); `:mono` (default) puts the input (a stereo input's average) into the left line, so echoes go L, R, L, ... each `feedback` times the last; `:stereo` feeds each side its own line; `spread:` is a `width` of the echoes (0 = every echo centered); `mix:` as for `#chorus`.
- `haas(time = 15.ms, side: :right, level: 1)`: one side delayed (precedence widening); stereo inputs keep the other side.
- `tape_echo(preset = :reel, time:, feedback:, mix:, tone:, low_cut:, drive:, wow:, flutter:, stereo: :mono | :ping_pong, spread:, seed:)` (`GraphNode::TapeEcho`, Presettable family `:delay`, `L.fx.delay.dub`; presets `:reel` (3/16, warm), `:slapback` (110 ms), `:ping_pong` (1/8, stereo), `:dub` (3/8, dark and saturated); the help effects topic plays `:dub`): a feedback loop `(input + g * fb).delay(time + wobble)` through a highpass (`low_cut:`), a lowpass (`tone:`), and `(x * drive).softclip / drive` on every pass; wow is a `drift` at 0.7 Hz and flutter a 6.5 Hz sine, both in ms, added to the delay time (Durations follow the tempo through `Sequence::TempoNode.seconds_source`); delay smoothing off (see below).  Loops use `compensate: :dc`: the `:pitch` mode measures the loop phase at 1 / time, a few Hz for an echo, where a highpass leads by up to 180 degrees, so repeats landed up to half an echo late (measured on master-ai 8c7c1c24: `delay(0.1, feedback: 0.5) { |fb| fb.filter(200.hz.highpass(quality: 0.5)).filter(3000.hz.lowpass(quality: 0.5)) }` put its first echo 2321 samples late, the second 2361 early; :dc within a few samples); `#delay`'s insert loops use `compensate: :echo` since omnibus16 (the same echo times as :dc, see feedback_loops.md; `bin/effects/tape_delay.rb`'s and feedback_song's echoes moved onto their times, Bench set omnibus16), and tape echoes keep `:dc`, cheaper while the wobbled time moves.  Echo peaks land within about 1 ms of k * time (the filters' shape).  `:ping_pong` is one loop of `2 * time` through two tape passes (right repeats; both wobbles in the one delay, so compensation stays exact) and a third pass outside the loop for the left repeats (a loop's inner nodes can't be read outside it).  Stereo inputs in `:mono` get two tapes (seeds `seed` and `seed + 1`).
- Found while building it (pre-existing): `#delay` with a node time and the default smoothing starts the time at 0 and glides it up at the smoothing rate (a 0.375 s wobbled time read 1200-1600 samples in the first buffers), so a short input's loop rang out before its first echo; tape echoes pass `smooth: false`.
