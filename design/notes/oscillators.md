# Oscillators and antialiasing

Detailed notes moved from CLAUDE.md (2026-10-10); CLAUDE.md keeps a summary and points here.  Update both when behavior changes.


Tone waveforms are antialiased by default (`lib/mb/sound/band_limit.rb`, kernels in the `fast_synth` extension); every shape has a naive (aliased) `a*` twin for lo-fi sounds and control signals.  Measure any expression with `bin/aliasing.rb 'p.ramp' 'p.aramp'` (coherent-sampling aliasing table and cost; `--render DIR` writes 100 Hz-8 kHz sweeps with spectrograms).

- `ramp`/`saw`, `square`, `triangle` use PolyBLEP/PolyBLAMP (corrections on the samples either side of each edge, found on the phase plus phase modulation, so FM/PM and through-zero motion work); `aramp`/`asaw`, `asquare`, `atriangle` are naive (`Tone#band_limited?`).  `.lfo` fades band-limiting in from 15 to 30 Hz (`BandLimit::LFO_FADE`), so slow LFOs keep exact jumps (delay times, gates) and audio-rate "LFOs" are clean.  Free-running PolyBLEP shapes have exact means at every pitch (PolyBLEP's step correction sums to the ideal step's area, and PolyBLAMP's extra 1/12 per corner cancels over a cycle): measured 2026-10-08 at 2-12 kHz, means within 1e-5 at any start phase, and 50 ms window means over a 2-12 kHz sweep within 8e-5 of the ideal band-limited waveform's (low-frequency beats of aliases near 0 Hz, -70 dB at 4799.7 Hz); a band-limited tone's first sample is corrected as if it had always run (`state.blep[3]` 0 = never played, 2 = after a jump), so a square starting at phase 0 plays 0, the ideal, not +1 (fixed 2026-10-08).
- `pwm(width)` (alias `skew`; width a number or node) warps the phase of any shape: `square` → pulse (`pulse(w)`/`apulse(w)`), `triangle.skew(0.1)` → near-saw, `sine.pwm(w)` → CZ-style phase distortion; DC offset removed by default (`dc: true` keeps it); the warp's corners are band-limited too.
- `complex_ramp`/`complex_square`/`complex_triangle` are closed-form band-limited impulse trains, integrated (`BandLimit.blit_ruby`): no aliasing or negative frequencies (-153 dB), top octave lifted ~2.6 dB.  With phase modulation, `pwm`, or sync (or resets and timelines with `clean`) they play from complex wavetables of the exact series (`Tone.complex_table`, `Tone#complex_table?`, kernel `:wavetable`; also `complex_sine` with a warp or sync; 2026-10-08, before: the naive `acomplex_*` with PM, `pwm` ignored, sync refused); without `clean`, resets of plain complex tones restart the impulse trains as before.  With `clean` (and no PM), warps, resets, and timeline jumps run through the synced table kernel without sync events (`Tone#clean_table?`: exact corners, resets as hard sync events with `FastWavetable.sync`'s `reset_phase`).  Measured two-sided (`bin/aliasing.rb -c`, harmonics on both sides): PM 0.5 rad (0.08 cycles) at 1 / 3 kHz -45 / -29 dB (naive -20 / -14; the rest are PM sidebands past Nyquist), `pwm(0.3)` -31 to -40 (naive -12 to -18; clean -113 to -118), sync 2.37 -101 to -112; clean resets against the ideal complex reset waveform -30 to -57 dB (restart -24 to -32; a minBLEP step on the table measured worse at edges, where the imaginary part has log peaks).  The impulse trains' phase leads the exact series by their integrators' leak (0.6 samples at 100 Hz, 0.006 at 1 kHz).
- `sync(ratio: r)` hard-syncs a tone to a hidden master at its own pitch (playing at pitch × r; r a number or node), `sync(master)` to a Pitch/Tone/phasor (its `wraps` port) or any trigger node; `softsync` reverses direction instead.  Synced tones use a causal minBLEP kernel whose output is exactly the naive waveform through the minBLEP's minimum-phase filter (`FastSynth.oscillate_sync`, `BandLimit.sync_tables`): polynomial segments are delayed by the filter's moments (about 2.78 samples) and events add residuals of their value, slope, and curvature jumps; sine segments use per-frequency residuals (`BandLimit.sync_sine_table`, 6 MB, built on first use).  So DC and harmonics match the ideal synced waveform (DC error < 1e-4; harmonics -56 to -106 dB; aliasing -88 to -135 dB); until 2026-10-07 every step left 2.78 samples times its height of DC behind (+0.82 on a ramp synced at 2.37x of 3 kHz) and soft-synced squares spiked on their edge at phase 0.  Synced sines cost 1.3-2.2% of realtime at 3-4 kHz masters; no phase modulation with sync.
- `fm_feedback(amount, gain: env, dc: false)` (aliases `fmfb`, `fm_fb`, `fm_feedback_cycles`/`fmfb_cycles`/`fm_fb_cycles`; amounts in cycles; sines only; renamed from `feedback`/`fb` 2026-10-09 so `feedback` is graph feedback, see Feedback loops) is FM operator self-feedback in the kernel (`FastSynth.feedback_sine`, mirror `Tone#feedback_ruby`, history `state.feedback` = [y1, y2, dc]): y = sin(2π(phase + pm + amount × avg(y[n-1], y[n-2]))) × gain, DX7-style two-sample average; `gain:` (an envelope) is inside the loop so brightness follows it, `at` is outside.  Measured at 55-440 Hz: 0.16-0.24 cycles (1-1.5 rad) saw-like, 0.32 (2 rad) the brightest clean setting; chaos starts ~0.35 but stays above 12 kHz (a hiss many playback chains drop) until ~0.56, audible broadband noise from ~0.64; `Tone::FEEDBACK_MAX` = 1 cycle (2π; DX7 FB 7 at full level) is the knob range (no clamping anywhere).  DC (mean -0.25 at 0.32 cycles) is removed from the output by default (`dc: true` keeps it) by a pitch-tracking one-pole at f/20 outside the loop.  Resets (key sync) clear the history so every note starts identically (`reset(t, keep_feedback: true)` / `.keep_feedback` keep it; timeline jumps keep it).  Aliases like FM at high notes (1 kHz, 0.24 cycles: worst alias -59 dB); ~28 ns/sample for any amount (sine 21).  Not with other shapes, pwm, sync, noise.  `Tone.dx7_feedback(0..7)` = 2^(fb-7) cycles (MSFA's arithmetic; the operator's level goes in `gain:`).  Demo: `bin/synths/fm_brass.rb` (`--feedback 0` to compare).
- `tone.gain(g)` (alias `amp`; number or node, e.g. an envelope) multiplies the output inside the Tone after `at` and jump steps, bit-identical to `tone * g` without a Multiplier (`FastArithmetic.scale`, Numo fallback/mirror); saves ~4 ns/sample at 128-sample buffers (~2 at 512) against `tone * env`.  On feedback sines it is the output gain; `fm_feedback(gain:)` is the in-loop one.
- Phase jumps (reset inputs, timeline jumps of tempo tones) are band-limited steps over the next 32 samples by default (minBLEP/minBLAMP from the old waveform to the new, queued in `state.jump_residual`; `Tone.jump_residual` adds a 4-tap raised cosine 1.5 samples after the jump so each step has the area of an ideal step on the sample, -dv/2 + ds/12: no DC drift at audio-rate resets; a reset on an edge in value starts from its left side; 2026-10-07; plain sines still jump naively).  Opt-in `clean` (`tone.clean`, `reset(trig, clean: true)`, `pwm(w, clean: true)`; user's choice 2026-10-08: the default keeps key-synced voices' cost predictable) plays a tone's jumps through the synced kernels: band-limited ramps, squares, triangles, and warped shapes without PM, noise, or an LFO fade (`Tone#reset_sync?`, kernel `:reset_sync`) make each jump a hard sync event on its sample to the target phase (`FastSynth.oscillate_sync`'s `reset_phase`), as clean as sync (harmonic error against the ideal reset waveform -72 to -86 dB instead of -19 to -38), at the cost of the naive waveform through the minimum-phase filter (about 2.8 samples of delay, minBLEP edge ringing with peaks up to ~1.4x PolyBLEP's) and about 2x the oscillator's CPU (bin/synths/fifth_pad.rb with `.clean` saws: 16% -> 19.5% of realtime at 512-sample buffers).  Resets happen on whole samples (sync pulses carry sub-sample times), so audio-rate resets jitter by up to a sample where sync doesn't.
- Reset inputs (top-down note flow): `tone.reset(trig, to: nil)` jumps the phase at every nonzero sample of `trig` (e.g. `clip.trigger`), at exactly that sample (step measured with that sample's FM/PM/width); `to:` is nil (the `with_phase` start), cycles, a node of cycles, or `:random`; `reset(nil)` removes it; not with `sync`.  The Tone splits the buffer at reset samples in Ruby around the kernels (a scan costs ~3 us per 128-sample buffer, each reset ~35 us; tones without a reset input skip it).  `.free` (`free?`) = never reset (no key sync in synth voices; reset+free and rnd+fixed `to:` conflicts: last call wins with a warning; reset+sync raises; `lfo` tones aren't `free?` but may take a reset input); `.random_phase`/`.rnd(seed:)` = random start, new random phase per reset.  Randomness: `MB::Sound.seed(n)` / `random_seed` / `next_seed` (`RandomMethods`, default seed 0 or `RANDOM_SEED`, restored before every spec example); random-phase tones draw a sub-seed when `rnd` is called (creation order), `Tone#seed=` for derived seeds.  `smooth(t, reset: trig)` jumps instead of gliding on resets.  Noise (`noise(seed:)`, `Tone#noise(blend, seed:)`) draws a sub-seed when `noise` is called too and keeps its own splitmix64 state (`Tone::State#noise`, read and written by the `fast_sound` kernels; exact Ruby mirror `Tone.noise_random`), so noise repeats from the seed and parts don't share a stream (it used C's process-wide `drand48` until 2026-10-06, so noise renders changed then); noise tones advance a running phase wrapped every sample (all four C kernels and both Ruby mirrors), so their output is the same in any block sizes (2026-10-10: before, ~1% of samples differed by an ulp, since increments reach ±freq/2 cycles and each block summed them from its start).
- `softclip`, `clip`, `abs`, numeric `quantize` use antiderivative antialiasing on the excess over the linear part plus a half-sample Thiran allpass on the dry signal (`MB::Sound::Shaper`, `GraphNode::Shaper`, the `fast_clip` extension): flat level, half a sample of delay, aliases below the fundamental 25-56 dB lower; `asoftclip`, `aclip`, `aabs`, `aquantize` are the plain shapers (exact, for control signals).
- Ports (`GraphNode::Ports`): side outputs that aren't audio channels, made on first use: `tone.wraps` (sync pulses valued 1 - d, d the sub-sample position of the wrap; a wrap within 1e-9 of a sample gives 1 on it, so 2000 Hz pulses every 24 samples; `BandLimit.sync_pulses`) and `.increment`, `TempoNode#jumps`/`#jump_phase` (see Tempo sync); `node.ports`, `port_info`, `inputs`.  A node with ports computes frames; every reader (main output and ports) must read each buffer, as a Session does.
- Wavetables (`lib/mb/sound/wavetable.rb`, `wavetable/`; kernels in `fast_wavetable`, exact mirrors in `Wavetable::KernelRuby`): `Wavetable` is one value class for cycle tables (frames, `scan:` 0..1 morphs linearly with frame k of N at k/(N-1), clamped, or with `scan_wrap: true` (Tone#wavetable, `phase_table`, `waveshape`) wrapping every N/(N-1): from 1 to 1 + 1/(N-1) the last frame morphs into the first at the usual rate, so a steady ramp loops the timbre seamlessly; frames aligned in time by default) and samples (`mode: :sample`, `root:`, `loop:` Range of samples or Lengths, one-shots end unless reset); perceived loudness: `Wavetable::Loudness` (an LUFS-like K-weighted power of the harmonics, power-averaged over C2-C6, from the spectra; TODO: switch to the lufs branch's BS.1770 tool), `#loudness`, `normalize: :loudness` on `.from_harmonics`/`.from_samples` scales every frame to the library saw's loudness (saved as a `normalize` tag; frames are saved scaled), and the library's non-classic tables use it (`:organ`, `:pulses`, and `:basic_norm`, the loudness-normalized twin of the exact `:basic` scan; 2026-10-08: the pulses' frames spread 7.3 dB before, rendered 0.09 dB after; the 3% pulse frame now peaks at 2.9, no gain cap: limiting comes later) while `:sine`/`:saw`/`:square`/`:triangle`/`:basic` stay exact; constructors `.from_harmonics` (exact Fourier amplitudes; the library's classic shapes are exact series too, with natural Gibbs peaks of about 1.18 on the saw and square, at the PolyBLEP tones' RMS and harmonic levels but with different peaks (user's choice; the `taper: :sigma` option was removed 2026-10-08, preferring the bright top octave, and files saved with a taper tag load untapered with a warning)), `.from_samples`, `.from_function`, `.from_file`, `Wavetable[:saw | 'file' | array]` (library: `.register`, `.names`); `#save` writes everything derived (`#metadata`: mode, period, frames, aligned, spacing, harmonics, root, loop, slicing details in `#source_info`) and `.from_file` uses it (no re-alignment; files hold frames, not levels, so levels are rebuilt on load: a spacing given explicitly with `mips:` is kept (`spacing_explicit`), a default one gets today's default, and `from_file(mips:)` re-mipmaps); the frame helpers are `Wavetable.load_frames`/`save_frames`/`slice_frames`/`normalize`/`sort`/`blur`.  Stored as levels (mipmaps, `mips:` `:half_octave` default in both modes (`Wavetable::DEFAULT_MIPS`; user's choice 2026-10-07: a saw sweep's 10-20 kHz band dips 0.7 dB instead of 2.8 with `:octave`); kernels always read two levels (weight 0 outside crossfades, bit-identical), so every pitch costs the same (user: predictability), `:octave`, a ratio, harmonic counts, or false) 4x oversampled; a tone picks levels by its motion per sample (increment, PM, warp slope) under the alias ceiling (harmonics may pass Nyquist if they fold above 20 kHz), crossfading over the last 1/5 octave (`LEVEL_FADE`).  Interpolation `:optimal` (Niemitalo 4-point 4th-order for 4x, default; its passband droop is undone by emphasized levels, `Wavetable::Emphasis`), `:cubic` (default without levels), `:linear`, `:none`, `:sinc` (Kaiser beta 12).  `440.hz.wavetable(t, scan:)` is a Tone wave type: FM, PM, `pwm` (PolyBLAMP corners; with `clean`, exact corners and resets through the synced table kernel, -106 to -115 dB instead of -31 to -41 at ~4x the cost, `Tone#clean_table?`), `sync`/`softsync` (real or complex cycle tables, and `pwm` with them: each sample sums the harmonics with every harmonic filtered by the minBLEP's H(h g) (`FastWavetable.sync`; Clenshaw sums in 4 chains, coefficients cached while the pitch and selection stay put), and every sync event and warp corner switches each harmonic with its unnormalized residual from `BandLimit.sync_sine_table` (the synced sines' table; `Wavetable.sync_filter`), so the output is exactly h applied to the naive synced table: DC within 1e-4 of the ideal over sweeps, and a one-harmonic table plays bit-identically to `sine.sync`; on `#sync_levels`: every harmonic count to 8 then quarter octaves, all below 20 kHz, always crossfading, so brightness doesn't step; truncated Taylor residuals overshot to several times full scale near 3 kHz), `noise`, resets, ports, `Wavetable::KeyMap` key zones picked at resets (`normalize: :loudness` scales each zone's table to the zones' mean perceived loudness at the middle of its zone, sample-mode zones included; `KeyMap.new(C1...C3 => :saw, ...)` still takes braceless zones).  `node.phase_table(t, scan:, wrap:, increment:)` (also `#wavetable` on non-Tones) reads at a phase in cycles and `sig.waveshape(t, scan:)` maps -1..1 across the table (clamped); both pick levels from an `increment:` node, a phasor's `increment` port, or else the input's own change per sample (held peak; `increment: false` = brightest level).  `Pitch#harmonics(spectrum, update: 512)` (alias `additive`; `GraphNode::HarmonicTable`) is an additive oscillator whose spectrum (numbers, nodes, or a callable of time) changes while it plays (32 harmonics: ~100 us per rebuild, 1.4% of realtime at 512-sample buffers, 1.8% at 128, plus its amplitude sources; spectra and half means in C, `FastWavetable.harmonic_spectra`/`.half_means`).  Table sync redesign 2026-10-08 (the 2026-10-07 study on the never-merged branch `research-sync` (2f595c60, `research/sync/`) found the old residuals, normalized by H per harmonic while the reads weren't filtered, drifted the mean up to 0.21 from the ideal, which blocked the warp corners).  Measured: saw table -104 to -110 dB of aliasing (NHR<20k) vs -33 to -38 for PolyBLEP `ramp`; synced tables -100 to -126 dB at 250 Hz-3 kHz masters, `pwm(0.3)` included (was -28 to -62); the synced kernel costs O(harmonics): 85 ns per sample at a 2.4 kHz slave, 220 ns at 150 Hz (the old one 55-75 at every pitch), recomputing every harmonic's H each sample while the pitch glides (150 ns at 2.4 kHz, ~1 us at 150 Hz; a future option: block-interpolate H every 16 samples while gliding, ~2.5x faster at an error near -100 dB, not built per the user) and at each warp corner (pwm 0.3: 270-600 ns); unsynced kernel 17-25 ns per sample; ~0.15-0.24% of realtime per tone.  Demo and snippets: `bin/songs/wavetable_song.rb`.
- Still aliasing: FM/PM sidebands and operator feedback (use `oversample`), `gauss`, naive `a*` shapes, unmipped (`mips: false`) tables.  Demo and A/B snippets: `bin/songs/antialias_song.rb`.
- Unison: `pitch.unison(n, detune: 12.cents, layout: :random, phase: :random, spread: 0, normalize: :power, seed: nil) { |p, i| p.saw }` on any Pitch (`220.hz`, Notes, `v.hz`; `lib/mb/sound/unison.rb`): the block gets each copy's `pitch.transpose(offset)` (so `v.hz` keeps key sync, bend, glide, vibrato), and every oscillator made from it (or pitches derived from it) gets the phase through `Pitch#unison_phase` (`:random` = `rnd`, new random phases at each key-sync reset; cycles; `:reset` = leave alone).  `detune` is the outermost copies' distance either side (Interval or semitones, or an Array of fixed offsets, or a node of semitones: copies keep fixed layout fractions -1..1 scaled by it, computed for all copies at once by `Unison::Detune` in the `fast_unison` extension; copies are `Unison::CopyPitch`es making the base pitch's kind of tone; `detune_mode: :exact` (default since 2026-10-07; every copy exp per sample, ~0.15% of realtime more for 7 copies) or `:interp` ramps the exact outermost ratio every 16 samples of the stream (buffer-size independent) and spaces copies linearly in Hz, middle copy sharp by 1200·log2(cosh(d·ln2/12)) cents (0.18 at 25 cents, 2.9 at 100), ~half the detune math of `:exact`; fixed detunes keep the plain `transpose` path); `:random` layout jitters by up to 1/3 step, recenters, rescales (seeded from the root RNG, or `seed:` for the layout only; per-lane in synths).  `GraphNode::ChannelMixer::Unison` mixes with 1/sqrt(n) (or `:peak` 1/n); `spread > 0` (or a node) returns stereo, equal-power pans × sqrt(2) with sides alternating by pair (`Unison.pan_slots`).  `mix:` (0..2 or a node, clamped; default 1 = bit-identical) sets the side copies' level to mix² (0.5 = -12 dB, 2 = +12 dB): center copies (`Unison.center_copies`: nearest the pitch for odd counts, two nearest for even; 2 copies are both centers) stay at 1, and the normalization follows the weights (power 1/sqrt(Σw²), so loudness stays put).  Per copy: the block gets `|p, i|`, `Pitch#unison_copy` (`Unison::Copy`: index, count, RNG from the unison's seed drawn after the layout), and pitch methods on copies take per-copy values: a Range (random), `spread(a..b)` (even, lowest copy first), `channels(...)` (cycled); Intervals → semitones, Lengths → seconds.  Node-detune copies of Notes pitches take `glide`/`bend_range`/`vibrato`/`transpose(node)` (`CopyPitch#notes_pitch`: a NotePitch transposed by the copy's offset node fraction × detune, so exact detune through `Notes::Frequency`; the `Detune` node is built lazily by `Unison::CopySet` only if plain copies use it).  Glides with the same settings share one `Notes::Glide` per Notes (memoized in `NotePitch#number_node`; before and inside the block alike).  `Pitch#transpose`/`NotePitch#transpose` take a node of semitones; a fixed transpose of a tempo pitch gives `TempoNode#scaled(ratio)` (exact ratio, freewheel follows the parent), so `1.beat.hz.unison(3)` stays timeline-locked.  `Notes::Glide` has `overshoot:` (0..1, smoothstep + k t³(1-t)²) and relative `from: -7.st`.  `pitch.swarm(12, chord:, detune:, glide: 0.08..0.9, overshoot:, scatter: 1.oct | from: G3..G4, drift: 10.cents, drift_rate:, spread: 1, ...)` (`lib/mb/sound/unison/swarm.rb`) is a unison of per-copy glides on Notes pitches, optional chord tones (`Unison.chord_offsets`), scattered or banded starts, and sine drift; cost ~0.8% of realtime per gliding copy at 512-sample buffers (Glide + Frequency + saw each; 24 copies ~20%, ~56% at 128), less once glides settle.  Demos: `bin/songs/unison_song.rb`, `bin/songs/swarm_song.rb`.
